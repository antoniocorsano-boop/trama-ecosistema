# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — Curriculum & Metaphor Decision Matrix

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery  
**Status:** EVIDENCE-BOUND WORKING MATRIX  
**Approval:** NONE  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED

## Purpose

Prevent Atlas Percorsi from becoming either an autonomous pseudo-curriculum or a visually attractive narrative detached from learning. This matrix connects the six candidate experience territories to authoritative curriculum/evidence families and establishes criteria for selecting a metaphor only after pedagogical, developmental, accessibility, privacy and sustainability checks.

## Source families used in this iteration

- Italian National Guidelines adopted by D.M. 221/2025, including the Student Profile, general educational aims, curriculum organisation and the gradual implementation provisions from school year 2026/27.
- European Commission/JRC LifeComp, a non-prescriptive conceptual framework for personal, social and learning-to-learn competence.
- Research on basic psychological needs and learner control in primary/middle/secondary education.
- Developmental research on metaphor comprehension.
- Existing CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI evidence, CHILD-SAFE and attention/cognitive-load constraints.

The matrix is an interpretation layer. It does not claim that the six Atlas territories are official curriculum categories.

## Curriculum relationship rule

Atlas Percorsi must use curriculum frameworks as **authority and traceability sources**, not as a pretext to create a parallel curriculum.

The mapping therefore distinguishes:

1. **authoritative curriculum anchor** — what the Italian framework expects or enables;
2. **competence lens** — conceptual support such as LifeComp;
3. **Atlas experience territory** — an organisational design hypothesis;
4. **evidence produced by the learner** — action/choice/artefact/reflection, not a personality score.

## Candidate mapping

| Atlas territory | Curriculum relationship to verify/finalise | LifeComp / research lens | Candidate evidence in Atlas | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Me** | Student development, self-knowledge, responsibility and orientation within the Student Profile/general formative aims | Personal area; self-regulation; well-being-related constructs only as educational lenses | contextual choice, recognition, brief reflection, learner-owned artefact | no psychological profile, diagnosis or trait score |
| **Me as a learner** | Learning-to-learn, study/strategy development, autonomy and progressive responsibility | LifeComp learning-to-learn; SRL/metacognition | strategy choice, plan/revision, explanation of what helped, transfer to another task | no global intelligence/ability judgement |
| **Me and others** | Participation, communication, coexistence, collaboration and citizenship across curriculum | LifeComp social area; relatedness evidence | perspective comparison, collaborative decision scenario, communication artefact | no social ranking or peer popularity metric |
| **Me facing problems** | Problem solving, critical/creative reasoning and integrated disciplinary situations | learning-to-learn plus competence/agency research | compare options, justify a decision, revise after consequence, transfer | no reward for impulsive speed; no single “correct personality” |
| **Me in the world** | Citizenship, responsibility, sustainability and informed participation | social competence; LifeComp; relevant competence frameworks where applicable | consequence map, responsible choice, source/evidence reasoning, local action plan | no political persuasion, behavioural profiling or unnecessary external disclosure |
| **Me as planner/agent** | Orientation, initiative, goal setting and responsible action | autonomy/competence/agency; LifeComp | learner-chosen goal, steps, revision, evidence of action and reflection | choice must be bounded and scaffolded; no coercive engagement |

### Interpretation

The six territories are **navigation and experience architecture**, not six subjects and not six official competences. A pathway may legitimately cross several territories. Arena remains the candidate authority source for governed curriculum bindings; the exact contract belongs to G2.

## Agency and choice — evidence implications

Research on self-determination-based educational interventions supports autonomy, competence and motivation, but it does not justify unlimited learner choice. Reviews of learner-controlled instruction show positive motivational effects but mixed cognitive outcomes, with age/context differences.

Therefore Atlas should implement **bounded meaningful choice**:

- choices must change something educationally meaningful;
- the number of simultaneous options should remain cognitively manageable;
- younger learners receive stronger structure and clearer consequences;
- lower-secondary learners can receive greater authentic agency without removing scaffolding;
- choice must never be simulated merely to create engagement;
- the learner must be able to understand the consequence of a choice before committing where the consequence matters.

## Metaphor decision protocol

A metaphor is acceptable only if the literal educational experience remains understandable without decoding the metaphor.

Each candidate metaphor must be assessed on the following dimensions before selection:

| Dimension | Required question | Reject/repair signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Pedagogical fit** | Does it naturally support awareness → choice → action → reflection → transfer? | metaphor rewards collection/completion instead of competence use |
| **Developmental fit** | Can different first-cycle age bands understand it without excessive inferential burden? | meaning depends on abstract figurative interpretation |
| **Cognitive economy** | Does it reduce orientation cost and preserve stable anchors? | continuous novelty, scene replacement or decorative complexity |
| **Agency** | Does it make meaningful choices legible? | fake choices, coercive paths or opaque consequences |
| **Non-infantilisation** | Can the language mature from primary to lower-secondary? | childish tone that cannot scale with age |
| **Accessibility** | Is there a literal, low-stimulation, reduced-motion equivalent? | essential meaning exists only in image, motion, sound or figurative language |
| **Privacy/safety** | Can it work with no account/profile/tracking and no free disclosure requirement? | narrative asks learner to reveal identity, private life or sensitive information |
| **Transfer** | Does it connect the symbolic experience back to real contexts? | learner remembers the fictional mechanic but not the competence/action |
| **Sustainability** | Can atmosphere be achieved without unnecessary heavy assets/computation? | visual spectacle becomes the primary value or requires avoidable resource use |
| **Teacher legibility** | Can an educator understand the pedagogical purpose without interpreting hidden game mechanics? | experience is pedagogically opaque to adults and learners |

## Candidate metaphor families for research — not selections

### M1 — Journey / paths / crossroads

**Potential:** naturally represents movement, choices, revisiting, orientation and transfer.  
**Risk:** extremely common; can become generic, linear or completion-driven.  
**Research question:** can it become distinctive through stable places/symbols without turning into a sequence of screens?

### M2 — Workshop / making / instruments

**Potential:** competence as something exercised, combined, tested and refined; strong action orientation.  
**Risk:** may over-materialise internal/social processes or privilege “building” aesthetics.  
**Research question:** can tools represent strategies without becoming collectible badges?

### M3 — Observatory / lenses / changing viewpoints

**Potential:** strong fit for noticing, perspective, evidence and reflection.  
**Risk:** may overemphasise observation and underrepresent action/relationship.  
**Research question:** can observation always lead to choice/action rather than passive consumption?

### M4 — Ecosystem / connections / growth

**Potential:** relationships, interdependence, consequences, sustainability and non-linear development.  
**Risk:** “growth” can imply deterministic maturation; visual complexity can become high.  
**Research question:** can it avoid growth-as-score and maintain cognitive economy?

### M5 — Map / constellation of experiences

**Potential:** non-linear navigation, stable spatial memory, visible relationships between experiences.  
**Risk:** map density and symbolic abstraction may overload younger learners.  
**Research question:** can semantic zoom and age-sensitive simplification preserve orientation without hiding meaning?

No family is preferred at G1. Hybridisation is also not authorised until individual families are tested against the matrix.

## Developmental caution on metaphor

Evidence shows that even young children can understand age-appropriate novel metaphors in suitable tasks, but metaphor comprehension depends on language, world knowledge, analogy and other developing abilities. Studies with 8–10-year-olds also show poorer metaphor performance among weaker comprehenders.

Consequences for Atlas:

- metaphor may **support** meaning but cannot carry essential instructions alone;
- literal labels and actions remain available;
- age-appropriate usability testing must include learners with different comprehension profiles;
- figurative language is not used as an intelligence gate;
- misunderstanding the metaphor must not block progress through the learning experience.

## Attention and visual-memory constraint

The atmosphere must preserve **continuity before novelty**. For every scene/screen, the future script must identify:

1. the single dominant pedagogical focus;
2. stable visual anchors carried forward;
3. what changes and why;
4. the cognitive action expected after viewing;
5. how the learner can return/recover orientation;
6. whether the same meaning survives in low-stimulation/reduced-motion mode.

A new image is justified by a new pedagogical function, not by a need to keep attention occupied.

## G1 validation package required before metaphor selection

For each surviving metaphor candidate prepare:

- one primary-age low-complexity scene;
- one later-primary scene using the same grammar;
- one lower-secondary scene without infantilising tone;
- literal/non-metaphorical equivalent;
- reduced-motion/low-stimulation equivalent;
- disclosure/data-surface check;
- asset/network/resource budget hypothesis;
- comprehension test questions;
- transfer question: “What would this help you do in a different real situation?”;
- failure analysis and negative knowledge.

## Decision status

**No metaphor selected. No visual style selected. No implementation library selected.**

The next decision should be evidence-based elimination of unsuitable metaphor families, followed by low-fidelity comparative prototypes. Implementation technology is selected only after the experience grammar survives that comparison.
