## 1. Purpose

Propose a second, materially different Atlas Percorso to exercise a transversal design competence:

> **Progettare una soluzione tenendo insieme più vincoli, rendere espliciti i compromessi e rivedere la proposta quando cambia un requisito.**

This dossier is an authority proposal only. It does not register the pathway in Atlas, authorize implementation for students, or authorize runtime publication.

## 2. Why this pathway is needed

The first governed pathway, `PW-MISSING-INFORMATION-01`, centers on identifying missing relevant information before deciding. This candidate must prove that the Atlas Experience Engine and Percorsi factory can support a different cognitive structure rather than reproducing the same decision pattern with different content.

The intended primitive emphasis is:

```text
EXPLORE → CONNECT → BUILD → REFRAME → TRANSFER
```

The pathway therefore focuses on constraints, relations and revision of a constructed proposal rather than on identifying one missing datum.

## 3. Competence target and observable performance

### Competence target

Develop a bounded design proposal in response to a brief, identify relevant constraints, make trade-offs explicit, revise the proposal after a material requirement changes, and reuse the strategy in a different context.

### Observable task performances

A learner may demonstrate, within the presented task only:

- identifying two or more relevant constraints;
- recognising that improving one property can worsen another;
- selecting or assembling a candidate solution from bounded elements;
- explaining a trade-off using task evidence;
- revising the candidate when a requirement changes;
- applying the same constraint/trade-off strategy to a changed context.

### Claim ceiling

Completion supports only evidence about performance in the presented bounded tasks. It does **not** establish durable creativity, intelligence, maturity, problem-solving personality, environmental values, design talent, persistence, motivation or any psychological trait.

Transfer remains a probe. A successful changed-context scene does not by itself prove stable multi-context transfer.

## 4. Scenario boundary

The final implementation must use an ordinary, non-safety-critical design brief suitable for lower-secondary review. The learner should work with bounded, fictional task information and a finite set of components/options.

A candidate scenario may concern organising, protecting, transporting or making a simple school/community resource easier to use, provided that:

- failure has no real-world safety consequence;
- no medical, structural, electrical or emergency design judgement is required;
- no real personal biography or family circumstance is needed;
- essential meaning does not depend on a metaphor;
- the scenario can be replaced without changing the underlying competence kernel.

The exact narrative is a later pedagogical/editorial decision and is not authorized by this dossier.

## 5. Learning structure

The intended reasoning chain is:

```text
brief
  → inspect constraints
  → connect tensions between constraints
  → build/select a candidate
  → inspect consequences
  → receive a changed requirement
  → reframe/revise
  → transfer the strategy to a different brief
  → terminal summary
```

At least one revision must be meaningful: the changed requirement must force a real alteration of the candidate, not merely different explanatory copy.

## 6. Evidence and feedback model

Feedback is process-oriented and may use the Experience Engine categories:

- `EVIDENCE_SUPPORTED`
- `EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`
- `ALTERNATIVE_PLAUSIBLE`
- `MODEL_NEEDS_REVISION`
- `TRANSFER_SUCCESSFUL`

There is no hidden score, rank, preferred personality, “creative type” or moralized good/bad learner route.

Where multiple solutions satisfy the brief, the pathway must preserve legitimate alternatives and explain their different trade-offs rather than fabricate one universally correct answer.

## 7. Interaction and data-flow boundary

Version 1 requires:

- no learner account or identifier;
- no server-side learner response;
- no analytics/profiling;
- no free-text collection by default;
- no camera, microphone, location or contact access;
- no hidden adaptive model;
- state in volatile session memory unless a separately governed contract authorizes local-device resume;
- explicit restart;
- no student-facing network write.

Preferred interactions are bounded choice, ordering/selection from a finite component set, comparison, revision and consequence inspection. Any future free-text input is a material governance change and requires explicit necessity/minimisation review.

## 9. Accessibility boundary

Every essential BUILD interaction must have a keyboard-operable and non-drag-only equivalent. The implementation candidate must demonstrate:

- semantic native controls where suitable;
- deterministic focus movement;
- no information conveyed by colour alone;
- reflow/zoom without task loss;
- touch targets appropriate to mobile use;
- reduced-motion compatibility;
- an equivalent representation for any spatial/visual construction;
- presentation grammar changes that do not change competence target or completion evidence.

Automated checks do not replace human assistive-technology review.
