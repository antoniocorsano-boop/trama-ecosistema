## 1. Product intent

This screenplay turns the governed implementation target into an authored learner experience. It does not change the approved competence target and does not authorize student runtime.

The learner should experience a compact design cycle:

`BRIEF → COMPARE → MAKE A TRADE-OFF EXPLICIT → BUILD A CANDIDATE → REQUIREMENT CHANGES → REVISE → NAME THE METHOD → TRANSFER`.

The point is not to discover one “correct object”. The point is to make constraints visible, connect a design choice to more than one consequence, revise rather than defend an earlier choice, and transfer the method to a different design surface.

## 2. Subject / premise

A school has a small unused area of its courtyard. The challenge is to propose a **compact shared rest/study corner** that can be used by a small group during breaks or outdoor activities.

The learner receives a bounded fictional brief. The solution must balance four explicit constraints:

- **cost** — resources are limited;
- **durability** — the solution stays outdoors and should tolerate ordinary use;
- **resource use / reversibility** — avoid unnecessary material and prefer elements that can be repaired, reused or moved where feasible;
- **usability** — the space must remain practical and comfortable for different users.

The brief deliberately makes it impossible to maximise every constraint at once.

No real construction instruction, procurement advice or structural safety claim is provided. This is a reasoning/design exercise.

## 3. Narrative identity

This pathway uses a restrained **design studio / project board** identity, not Journey/Crossroads.

Recurring visual anchors:

1. **brief strip** — what the project must do;
2. **constraint board** — four stable constraints remain visible;
3. **candidate sheet** — selected solution and its consequences;
4. **change card** — one requirement changes;
5. **revision layer** — learner sees what is kept, changed and traded off;
6. **method card** — the reusable strategy abstracted from the project.

Visual language should feel closer to a design notebook/workbench than a game reward screen. No mascot is required. Motion is unnecessary.

## 4. Learner-facing scene sequence

### C1 — THE BRIEF / EXPLORE

**Purpose:** orient to the project and inspect the constraints before choosing.

Learner sees:
- the courtyard corner;
- the intended use;
- the four constraints;
- a literal statement that no option can maximise all four.

Prompt:
> “Prima di scegliere una soluzione, osserva i vincoli. Che cosa deve riuscire a fare il progetto?”

Interaction: deliberate continue after inspecting the brief.

No badge is earned by entering or leaving this scene.

### C2 — TWO CANDIDATES / CONNECT

Two deliberately plausible candidates are shown with equal visual salience.

**Candidate A — robust/modular**
- higher initial cost;
- better durability;
- less frequent maintenance;
- more material committed initially.

**Candidate B — lighter/economical**
- lower initial cost;
- easier initial setup;
- more frequent maintenance;
- shorter useful life for some elements.

Prompt:
> “Quale direzione vuoi sviluppare? Non cercare la soluzione perfetta: guarda quale compromesso stai accettando.”

Both routes are legitimate exploration routes. Choosing A or B does not earn a positive achievement.

### C3A / C3B — MAKE THE TRADE-OFF EXPLICIT / CONNECT

The candidate sheet remains visible.

For A, the learner chooses the statement that correctly expresses the trade-off:
- qualifying: “Accetto un costo iniziale maggiore per proteggere durata e manutenzione.”
- non-qualifying: “È la soluzione migliore perché dura di più.”

For B:
- qualifying: “Accetto più manutenzione per proteggere il budget iniziale.”
- non-qualifying: “È la soluzione migliore perché costa meno.”

The qualifying response earns the local evidence candidate:

**Traguardo:** “Rendo esplicito un compromesso di progetto.”  
**Stage:** `BEGINNING_TO_RECOGNISE`.

The non-qualifying response receives informational feedback and still allows the learner to continue; it does not award the traguardo.

### C4A / C4B — BUILD THE CANDIDATE / BUILD

The learner now chooses how to keep the selected candidate coherent with the full brief.

A:
- qualifying: retain the robust core but remove non-essential extras and keep the usability requirement visible;
- weak option: spend the remaining budget on decorative additions while leaving the resource constraint unresolved.

B:
- qualifying: use replaceable/modular elements and reserve part of the budget for expected maintenance;
- weak option: use the entire budget immediately and ignore maintenance.

Feedback explains effects on constraints. No personality or creativity label is inferred.

### C5A / C5B — A REQUIREMENT CHANGES / REFRAME

The project receives a new requirement.

For A:
> “Il budget disponibile si riduce del 25%.”

For B:
> “La manutenzione può essere programmata solo una volta all’anno.”

Prompt:
> “Che cosa conviene cambiare senza perdere la funzione principale?”

The learner chooses between:
- a qualifying revision that explicitly reacts to the new requirement while checking the remaining constraints;
- a non-qualifying “defend the original solution” choice.

The qualifying revision earns:

**Traguardo:** “Rivedo una soluzione quando cambia un requisito.”  
**Stage:** `CHOOSES_WHEN_TO_USE`.

### C6 — NAME THE METHOD / REFRAME

The two routes converge.

Prompt:
> “Quale metodo hai appena usato?”

Qualifying:
> “Rendo espliciti i vincoli, collego scelte e conseguenze, scelgo un compromesso e rivedo la soluzione se cambia un requisito.”

Non-qualifying:
> “Scelgo l’opzione che sembra migliore e cerco di mantenerla.”

This scene externalises the strategy. It does not add a new badge by itself.

### C7 — NEW DESIGN SURFACE / TRANSFER

New context: a school group must create a **portable exhibition stand** for a corridor/event. The object and immediate constraints differ: portability, available space, setup time, material reuse.

Prompt:
> “Che cosa trasferisci dal progetto precedente?”

Qualifying:
> “Riparto dai nuovi vincoli, collego alternative e conseguenze e sono pronto a rivedere la soluzione.”

Non-qualifying:
> “Copio la soluzione del cortile perché ha già funzionato.”

The qualifying transition earns:

**Traguardo:** “Trasferisco il metodo di progetto in una nuova situazione.”  
**Stage:** `TRANSFERS_TO_NEW_SITUATION`.

### C8 — TRACE / TERMINAL

The pathway ends with a compact method summary.

The learner may manage the already-governed local-growth record separately:
- local only;
- opt-in;
- exportable;
- resettable;
- no server synchronisation;
- no account, score, streak or ranking.

The terminal does not award an achievement merely for completion.

## 5. Feedback grammar

Feedback MUST describe relationships among constraints, consequences and revisions.

Allowed forms:
- “Questa scelta protegge X ma aumenta il costo su Y.”
- “Il nuovo requisito rende necessario rivedere Z.”
- “Hai trasferito il metodo, non la soluzione.”

Avoid:
- “bravo”, “geniale”, “sei creativo”;
- correct/incorrect colour coding as the only cue;
- moralising resource-use feedback;
- praise for simply choosing the more expensive, cheaper or more ‘sustainable-looking’ option.

## 6. Local-growth evidence mapping

| Evidence | Qualifying outcome | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| explicit trade-off | C3A qualifying OR C3B qualifying | BEGINNING_TO_RECOGNISE |
| revise after changed requirement | C5A qualifying OR C5B qualifying | CHOOSES_WHEN_TO_USE |
| transfer design method | C7 qualifying | TRANSFERS_TO_NEW_SITUATION |

Arrival at a scene, pathway completion, time-on-task and route choice alone are not achievement evidence.

## 7. Privacy / child-safety

Version 1 remains bounded-choice, client-only and non-identifying.

Forbidden:
- free text;
- name/class/student identifier;
- personal home/school disclosure;
- behavioural analytics;
- hidden score;
- creativity/personality inference;
- remote persistence;
- generative conversational agent.

## 8. Accessibility and attention

- stable constraint board throughout the design sequence;
- essential relationships expressed in text, not colour alone;
- keyboard-complete choices;
- focus moves to the new scene heading;
- no essential animation;
- mobile/reflow preserved;
- literal comprehension independent of the design-studio metaphor;
- no decorative visual churn around the constraint board.

## 9. Human review questions

Before integration as a product-authored candidate, review the exact learner-facing implementation for:

1. whether the school-design premise feels plausible and non-infantilising for lower secondary;
2. whether A and B are genuinely plausible rather than hidden right/wrong choices;
3. whether the four constraints are understandable without specialist prior knowledge;
4. whether qualifying trade-off statements require reasoning rather than keyword matching;
5. whether the changed requirement forces meaningful revision;
6. whether the transfer surface is materially different enough;
7. whether badge labels describe bounded evidence without overstating competence;
8. whether the experience remains engaging without reward pressure or visual overload.

This screenplay authorises no student use and no publication. It is a product-design candidate to be implemented and qualified on an exact Atlas head before Human Review.
