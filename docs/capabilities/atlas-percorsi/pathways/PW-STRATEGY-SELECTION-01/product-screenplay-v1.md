# PW-STRATEGY-SELECTION-01 — Product Screenplay v1

**Status:** PRODUCT_SCREENPLAY_CANDIDATE / NOT_RUNTIME_AUTHORIZED  
**Date:** 2026-10-04  
**Pathway:** `PW-STRATEGY-SELECTION-01 — Stesso obiettivo, strategia diversa`  
**Developmental band:** lower-secondary candidate / human validation required  
**Candidate territory:** `learning` / `CANDIDATE_NOT_APPROVED`  
**Product contract:** `../../product/STUDENT-LIBRARY-PRODUCT-CONTRACT-v1.md`

## 1. Product intent

This pathway develops one bounded metacognitive action:

> choose a strategy because it fits the current task, inspect evidence of fit, and change strategy when the task or evidence changes.

The experience must never imply that the learner has a fixed “study style”. Strategies are presented as tools selected for purposes.

Functional sequence:

`GOAL → CHOOSE → INSPECT CONSEQUENCE → CHECK FIT → GOAL CHANGES → REVISE → NAME PRINCIPLE → NEW TASK → APPLIED TRANSFER → TRACE`.

## 2. Subject / premise

The learner works in a quiet **strategy laboratory** with a fictional information sheet about a small expedition route.

The sheet contains five neutral facts:

1. the group begins at the field station;
2. it crosses a footbridge;
3. heavy rain makes the lower path unusable;
4. the group uses the ridge path instead;
5. it reaches the observation point later than planned.

No outside knowledge is needed.

Two different goals are applied to the same material:

- first, reconstruct the **order** of the route;
- later, explain the **cause–effect relation** that changed the route.

The same strategy is therefore not automatically optimal for both goals.

## 3. Narrative identity

Visual identity: restrained study-lab / workbench.

Stable anchors:

- **goal card** — current task;
- **information strip** — facts always available;
- **strategy tray** — bounded tools;
- **evidence window** — what the strategy made easier or left unresolved;
- **switch marker** — revision is legitimate;
- **method card** — abstract principle after use.

No mascot, avatar, personality graph, score, streak or essential animation.

## 4. Learner-facing sequence

### M1 — WHAT IS THE GOAL? / EXPLORE

The learner sees the five expedition facts and the first goal:

> “Tra poco dovrai ricostruire l’ordine del percorso senza guardare la scheda.”

Prompt:

> “Prima di scegliere un metodo, chiarisci che cosa dovrai riuscire a fare.”

Interaction: deliberate continue.

No growth evidence is awarded.

### M2 — CHOOSE A TOOL / CHOOSE

Three strategy cards are shown with equal visual status:

**A — Sequenza + recupero**
> “Metto i passaggi in ordine, copro la scheda e provo a ricostruirli senza guardare.”

**B — Mappa causa-effetto**
> “Collego gli eventi con frecce: che cosa provoca che cosa.”

**C — Rilettura continua**
> “Rileggo tutta la scheda più volte senza cambiare rappresentazione.”

Prompt:

> “Quale strategia useresti per prepararti a ricostruire l’ordine?”

For this goal, A is the qualifying task-fit choice. B is a useful strategy for another goal; C may feel familiar but provides weaker evidence for the explicit sequence-retrieval goal.

Choosing A alone does **not** award growth evidence. The pathway must first observe an applied sequence-retrieval action.

### M3A — TEST THE SEQUENCE / CONNECT

If A was chosen, the learner sees a compact reconstruction prompt:

> “Quale ordine useresti per controllare il tuo ricordo?”

Options present one correct sequence and plausible altered sequences.

The purpose is not factual assessment about expeditions; it is to make the consequence of the strategy inspectable.

Feedback:

> “Il metodo ti ha fatto esercitare proprio l’ordine che il compito richiedeva.”

Only a correct applied reconstruction after selecting/switching to the sequence-retrieval strategy earns:

**Traguardo:** “Collego una strategia allo scopo del compito.”  
**Stage:** `BEGINNING_TO_RECOGNISE`.

### M3B — THE GOAL IS STILL ORDER / CONNECT

If B or C was chosen, the evidence window says what the strategy supported and what remains weak.

For B:

> “Hai reso visibile perché il percorso è cambiato, ma il tuo obiettivo attuale è ricostruire l’ordine.”

For C:

> “Hai rivisto tutte le informazioni, ma non hai ancora verificato se riesci a recuperare l’ordine senza guardare.”

Prompt:

> “Che cosa faresti adesso?”

Qualifying revision:
> “Passo a una sequenza ordinata e provo a recuperarla senza guardare.”

Non-qualifying:
> “Continuo nello stesso modo anche se non ho ancora controllato l’ordine.”

A revision here is evidence of task-bound adjustment, not of intelligence or motivation.

### M4 — CHECK THE EVIDENCE / REFRAME

The goal card remains visible.

Prompt:

> “Che cosa ti dice l’evidenza?”

Qualifying:
> “Una strategia è utile se mi aiuta a fare ciò che il compito richiede; se non basta, posso cambiarla.”

Non-qualifying:
> “Se una strategia mi piace, dovrei usarla sempre.”

No new badge is awarded; this scene prepares the goal change.

### M5 — THE GOAL CHANGES / REFRAME

Same five facts. New goal:

> “Ora non devi ricordare l’ordine. Devi spiegare perché il gruppo ha cambiato percorso.”

The learner must choose again:

**A — Continuo con la sequenza**
> useful for order, but not sufficient to expose the causal relation.

**B — Costruisco una mappa causa-effetto**
> heavy rain → lower path unavailable → ridge path → later arrival.

**C — Rileggo senza organizzare**
> may support familiarity but does not explicitly represent the requested relation.

Prompt:

> “Quale strategia serve adesso?”

Qualifying choice: B.

Selecting B alone does **not** award growth evidence.

### M6 — MAKE THE RELATION VISIBLE / BUILD

The learner applies the chosen strategy through a bounded representation task.

Prompt:

> “Quale schema rende visibile il motivo del cambiamento?”

Qualifying representation:
> “Pioggia intensa → sentiero basso non utilizzabile → percorso di cresta → arrivo più tardi.”

Distractors either reverse causality or list facts without relations.

This scene ensures the learner does not merely select the label “mappa causa-effetto”; the learner applies it to the material.

Only the correct applied cause–effect representation after the goal change earns:

**Traguardo:** “Cambio strategia quando cambia lo scopo.”  
**Stage:** `CHOOSES_WHEN_TO_USE`.

### M7 — NAME THE PRINCIPLE / REFRAME

Prompt:

> “Quale principio hai usato?”

Qualifying:
> “Prima chiarisco il compito, poi scelgo una strategia; controllo se funziona e la cambio se lo scopo o l’evidenza cambiano.”

Non-qualifying:
> “Trovo il mio metodo preferito e lo uso in ogni situazione.”

This scene names a principle already exercised. It does not by itself award transfer evidence.

### M8 — NEW TASK, NEW REPRESENTATION / TRANSFER

New context: a fictional museum offers two visit routes.

**Route Blu**
- 45 minutes;
- 5 rooms;
- lift available;
- one hands-on station.

**Route Verde**
- 30 minutes;
- 3 rooms;
- stairs only;
- two hands-on stations.

New goal:

> “Devi confrontare i due percorsi rispetto a durata, accesso e attività pratiche per preparare una decisione.”

Prompt:

> “Quale lavoro concreto faresti per rendere il confronto controllabile?”

Qualifying applied choice:
> “Creo una tabella con una riga per ogni criterio e una colonna per ciascun percorso, poi confronto voce per voce.”

Non-qualifying:
> “Memorizzo l’ordine delle stanze del Percorso Blu.”

Choosing the comparison-table strategy is necessary but not sufficient.

### M8B — APPLY THE COMPARISON / BUILD

The learner sees three candidate working tables. Only one organises the supplied museum data by the stated criteria:

| Criterio | Blu | Verde |
|---|---|---|
| durata | 45 min | 30 min |
| accesso | ascensore | solo scale |
| attività pratiche | 1 | 2 |

Other options either mix criteria, omit one route, or reproduce an irrelevant sequence.

Prompt:

> “Quale tabella rende davvero controllabile il confronto richiesto?”

Only the correct applied representation earns:

**Traguardo:** “Scelgo e applico una strategia adatta in un nuovo compito.”  
**Stage:** `TRANSFERS_TO_NEW_SITUATION`.

This is a local transfer probe, not certification of general metacognitive competence.

### M9 — TRACE / TERMINAL

Summary:

> “Le strategie sono strumenti. Il punto non è trovarne una ‘tua’, ma scegliere quella che serve al compito, controllare l’effetto e cambiarla quando necessario.”

Local-growth record, if already enabled by the shared product layer:

- opt-in only;
- local device only;
- exportable/resettable;
- no account or remote synchronisation;
- no score, ranking or streak.

Completion alone awards nothing.

## 5. Feedback grammar

Allowed:

- “Questa strategia rende visibile l’ordine che devi recuperare.”
- “Questa scelta mostra le cause, ma il compito attuale chiede una sequenza.”
- “Lo scopo è cambiato: anche lo strumento può cambiare.”
- “Hai applicato il principio a un compito con struttura diversa.”

Avoid:

- “Sei bravo a studiare.”
- “Sei un tipo visivo.”
- “Non sai concentrarti.”
- “Hai trovato il tuo metodo.”
- “Questa è la strategia migliore in assoluto.”

## 6. Local-growth evidence mapping

| Evidence | Qualifying outcome | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| strategy–goal fit | M3A correctly reconstructs the sequence after selecting/switching to sequence + retrieval | BEGINNING_TO_RECOGNISE |
| strategic revision | M6 correctly applies a cause–effect representation after the goal change | CHOOSES_WHEN_TO_USE |
| applied transfer | M8B correctly applies the criterion-by-criterion comparison representation in the new museum task | TRANSFERS_TO_NEW_SITUATION |

A scene arrival, completion, time-on-task or initial preference is not growth evidence.

## 7. Privacy / child-safety

Version 1 is bounded-choice, client-only and non-identifying.

Forbidden:

- free text;
- name/class/student identifier;
- personal grades or study history;
- diagnosis or difficulty disclosure;
- behavioural analytics;
- hidden score;
- learning-style inference;
- remote persistence;
- generative conversational agent.

## 8. Accessibility and attention

- goal card remains visible;
- information sheet remains inspectable when memory is not the construct being tested;
- essential distinctions are textual, not colour-only;
- complete keyboard path;
- deterministic focus to new scene heading;
- no essential animation/audio;
- mobile reflow;
- strategy cards use parallel wording and equal visual salience;
- low-stimulation literal mode is fully equivalent.

## 9. Human product review questions

1. Is “strategy–task fit” understandable without suggesting a fixed learning style?
2. Are A/B/C presented neutrally enough that the learner must reason from the goal rather than visual cues?
3. Does M3 make the consequence of a strategy observable rather than merely telling the learner the answer?
4. Does M5 genuinely require revision because the goal changed?
5. Does M6 require application of the cause–effect representation?
6. Does M8B require an applied new-task representation rather than repetition of M7?
7. Are all three growth events tied to applied task performance rather than strategy-label selection?
8. Is the tone appropriate and non-infantilising for lower secondary?
9. Does the experience avoid unnecessary working-memory and navigation burden?
10. Does any wording invite trait inference or autobiographical disclosure?
