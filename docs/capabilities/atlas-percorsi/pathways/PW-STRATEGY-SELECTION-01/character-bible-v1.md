# PW-STRATEGY-SELECTION-01 — Character Bible v1

**Status:** CANDIDATE / HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED  
**Runtime:** NOT_RUNTIME_AUTHORIZED

Characters are cognitive/narrative actors. None represents a personality type or learner identity.

## Marta

**Scene role:** route/location carrier.

Knows:
- departure point;
- bridge and fork locations;
- which route the group physically takes.

Does not independently explain:
- why the lower path becomes unavailable;
- how the detour affects timing.

Voice:
- concise;
- concrete;
- refers to places/actions.

Example:
> “Siamo al bivio. Il sentiero basso è davanti a noi, la cresta sale a destra.”

Avoid:
- “the organised one”;
- authority/leader stereotypes.

## Samir

**Scene role:** weather/condition carrier.

Knows:
- rain intensifies;
- lower path is no longer usable;
- field conditions changed.

Does not independently explain:
- complete route chronology;
- final delay.

Voice:
- observational;
- matter-of-fact.

Example:
> “La pioggia è aumentata. Il sentiero basso non si può più usare.”

Avoid:
- “science kid” stereotype;
- technical jargon not needed by the learner.

## Nico

**Scene role:** time/outcome carrier.

Knows:
- planned observation window;
- arrival happens later than planned.

Does not independently explain:
- route mechanics;
- weather cause.

Voice:
- practical;
- short time/outcome references.

Example:
> “Siamo arrivati dopo l’orario previsto. Nel diario dobbiamo spiegare perché.”

Avoid:
- “anxious timekeeper” stereotype;
- blame or performance pressure.

## Learner

**Role:** fourth member of the reconstruction team.

The learner:
- combines the three partial viewpoints;
- organises the visual diary;
- helps change representation when the question changes.

The interface must never praise or criticise the learner's identity.

## Relationship

The group is cooperative. No conflict is needed to create engagement.

Narrative tension comes from:
- incomplete/disordered information;
- changing environmental conditions;
- the shift from chronology to explanation.

## Visual direction

Age: lower-secondary peer group.

Visual treatment:
- contemporary but non-fashion-coded;
- expressive enough to carry attention and viewpoint;
- no mascot/chibi infantilisation;
- no fixed colour/personality coding;
- clothing and appearance should remain ordinary and non-stereotyped.

Characters should recur across scenes with stable silhouettes, clothing and props so that the learner can recognise who carries which information.
