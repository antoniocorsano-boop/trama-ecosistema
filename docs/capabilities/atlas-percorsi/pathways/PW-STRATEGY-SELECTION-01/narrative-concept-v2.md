# PW-STRATEGY-SELECTION-01 — Narrative Concept v2

**Status:** NARRATIVE_REDESIGN_CANDIDATE / HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED  
**Working title:** Missione Belvedere  
**Runtime:** NOT_RUNTIME_AUTHORIZED

## Premise

A small fictional school field team is carrying out an environmental observation activity. The group wants to reach the Belvedere observation point in time to record a scheduled measurement.

During the route, heavy rain makes the lower path unavailable. The group diverts to a longer ridge route and arrives after the planned observation window.

The team's field diary contains several visual moments and short messages. After synchronisation, those moments are out of order.

The learner is not asked to “put five facts in order” as an abstract exercise. The learner joins the team to **rebuild the field diary so the class can understand what happened**.

## Learner role

The learner is a **member of the reconstruction team** working with the three fictional characters.

The learner:
- sees the same map/world as the characters;
- receives visual moments and character messages;
- rebuilds the story;
- later helps the team change representation when the question changes from “what happened?” to “why were we late?”.

The learner is not a judge, diagnostician, saviour or authority figure.

## Narrative engine

1. **Shared goal:** reach Belvedere for the scheduled observation.
2. **Progress:** the group moves through a visible route.
3. **Disruption:** weather changes.
4. **Consequence:** the lower path is unavailable.
5. **Adaptation:** the group takes the longer ridge route.
6. **Outcome:** arrival is late.
7. **Reconstruction problem:** diary moments are out of order.
8. **Goal shift:** after reconstructing chronology, the class asks for the cause of the delay.
9. **Representation shift:** chronology is no longer enough; the learner needs a causal representation.
10. **Principle:** different questions require different ways of organising the same information.

## Visual-narrative form

The primary material is not generic cards. It is a sequence of **illustrated story moments** in one coherent world:

- field station / departure;
- bridge;
- storm at the fork;
- ridge detour;
- Belvedere arrival.

Each moment combines:
- environment image;
- character presence;
- one short speech/message;
- one action or state change.

The map remains a continuity anchor, but characters and scenes carry meaning.

## Information distribution

Information is deliberately distributed across characters:

- Marta carries route/location information;
- Samir carries weather/field-condition information;
- Nico carries timing/outcome information.

No character alone explains the whole story. The learner's cognitive work is to combine viewpoints and then choose an appropriate representation.

## Emotional register

Low-stakes tension only:
- surprise at the weather change;
- mild frustration about the missed timing;
- satisfaction from reconstructing and explaining.

No fear, danger, injury, abandonment or emergency framing.

## First-cycle language

Characters speak in ordinary language:
- short sentences;
- no educational terminology;
- no “strategy”, “metacognition”, “transfer” or “competence” language;
- no exaggerated slang;
- no infantilising tone.

## Failure condition

This narrative concept fails if:
- characters can be removed without changing the task;
- the visual moments are just text cards with avatars added;
- the map and characters do not affect what the learner understands;
- the learner is still effectively guessing an arbitrary sequence;
- the narrative becomes longer than the cognitive work it supports.

## Transfer direction

A later changed-context task may keep the same cast but move to a genuinely different situation (for example planning/comparing two museum routes for a class visit), preserving social continuity while requiring a different representation.

That transfer remains a candidate and must retain the existing evidence standard: actual applied comparison, not label recognition.
