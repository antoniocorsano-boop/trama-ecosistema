# PW-STRATEGY-SELECTION-01 — World Concept Sprint v1

**Status:** PRODUCT EXPLORATION / HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED  
**Target competence:** choose/use/revise an information-organising strategy according to the task  
**Constraint:** no learner profiling, no runtime authorization, no Atlas implementation yet

## Evaluation criteria

Each candidate is screened for:

1. intrinsic hook;
2. learner role;
3. productive agency;
4. visual/world richness;
5. information distributed in the world;
6. visible consequences;
7. natural fit for multiple information strategies;
8. mobile 2-D feasibility;
9. age-appropriate dignity;
10. desire-to-continue potential.

Scores are directional product judgement, not empirical validation.

# Candidate A — NOVA-7 / Segnale fuori rotta

## Premise

A small exploration rover on a fictional moon missed a scheduled transmission.

The learner joins a compact mission-control crew trying to understand what happened before the next communication window.

## Learner role

Junior mission analyst.

## World

- mission-control room;
- terrain map;
- rover route;
- telemetry stream;
- short crew messages;
- instrument panels;
- image snapshots.

## Natural cognitive progression

First question:
> What happened, in what order?

Useful representation:
- timeline.

Second question:
> Why did the rover miss the transmission window?

Useful representation:
- cause/effect chain.

Third question:
> Which recovery route should we send?

Useful representation:
- comparison/constraint table.

## Agency

Learner can:
- inspect map/logs/data in chosen order;
- pin evidence;
- construct timeline;
- switch to causal map;
- compare recovery options;
- submit a recommendation.

## Consequence

The mission team changes the recovery plan based on the learner's model.

## Strengths

- strong visual identity;
- natural control-room tools;
- multiple representations feel authentic;
- high state visibility;
- excellent fit with strategy–task selection.

## Risks

- can feel technically cold;
- generic sci-fi aesthetic if under-authored;
- could repeat the exact failure of Missione Belvedere if characters remain information terminals.

## Directional score

- hook: 4/5
- agency: 5/5
- visual world: 5/5
- strategy fit: 5/5
- emotional/social investment: 3/5
- mobile feasibility: 4/5

**Overall:** strong mechanics, moderate warmth.

# Candidate B — MUSEO ZERO / La sala che non torna

## Premise

Tomorrow is the opening of a new interactive exhibition.

During the final evening test, one room behaves strangely: visitors' route, lights, sound and one interactive installation are no longer synchronised.

Nobody knows whether the problem comes from the order of setup, a changed room condition, or a configuration decision.

The learner joins the small youth setup crew to get the exhibition ready before opening.

## Learner role

New member of the exhibition crew.

## World

A compact illustrated museum:
- entrance hall;
- interactive room;
- control booth;
- storage/workshop;
- floor map;
- installation objects;
- crew messages;
- test recordings;
- visitor-flow simulation.

## Characters

Each character has a working responsibility rather than a personality label:

- **Lia** — spatial setup / visitor route;
- **Omar** — interactive installation / system state;
- **Teo** — timing / rehearsal observations.

They disagree occasionally because they saw different parts of the setup.

## Natural cognitive progression

First question:
> In what order did the setup changes happen?

Useful representation:
- timeline.

The learner needs this because the crew remembers events differently.

Second question:
> Which change actually caused the room to stop working correctly?

Useful representation:
- causal map.

The timeline is no longer enough.

Third question:
> Which fix should we try before opening?

Useful representation:
- comparison of fixes against constraints: time, visitor access, reliability.

## Agency

Learner can:
- walk between 3–4 locations;
- inspect objects;
- ask characters about specific moments;
- collect traces;
- build timeline;
- run a room test;
- see the failure;
- build causal explanation;
- choose/test a repair;
- watch room state change.

## Consequence

The room visibly changes:
- route/lights/installation become synchronised or remain problematic;
- crew reacts;
- opening status changes.

## Strengths

- visually rich but feasible in 2-D;
- combines people, space, objects and system state;
- natural “something is wrong” hook;
- strategies become tools for a real task;
- easy to vary rhythm: explore → talk → test → build → see consequence;
- not school-coded;
- no need for fantasy danger or high-stakes drama;
- strong foundation for future design/technology Percorsi.

## Risks

- requires higher illustration/art direction quality;
- must avoid turning the museum into a static hotspot tour;
- consequence model must be genuinely interactive.

## Directional score

- hook: 4.5/5
- agency: 5/5
- visual world: 5/5
- strategy fit: 5/5
- emotional/social investment: 4.5/5
- mobile feasibility: 4.5/5

**Overall:** strongest current candidate.

# Candidate C — RADIO 19 / Prima della diretta

## Premise

A youth radio/podcast team is preparing a live episode.

A field interview, sound log and location notes have become mixed up after an editing problem. The team needs to rebuild what happened and decide what explanation can actually be broadcast.

## Learner role

Junior producer/editor.

## World

- studio;
- soundboard;
- waveform clips;
- field map;
- message thread;
- interview fragments;
- running order.

## Natural cognitive progression

First:
- reconstruct sequence from clips/logs.

Second:
- distinguish chronology from explanation.

Third:
- compare two ways of presenting the story to listeners.

## Agency

Learner can:
- listen/inspect fragments;
- arrange running order;
- flag uncertain evidence;
- choose which clips support which claim;
- preview a short final segment.

## Consequence

The final episode changes according to the learner's structure.

## Strengths

- strong media literacy relevance;
- highly mobile-friendly;
- good use of sound;
- contemporary and socially plausible;
- easy to distribute information naturally.

## Risks

- audio increases accessibility/production complexity;
- causal structure may feel less intrinsic than in Museo Zero;
- can drift toward “editing exercise”.

## Directional score

- hook: 4/5
- agency: 4.5/5
- visual world: 4/5
- strategy fit: 4.5/5
- emotional/social investment: 4/5
- mobile feasibility: 5/5

**Overall:** strong alternate, especially for media/information-literacy pathways.

## Recommendation

Advance **MUSEO ZERO / La sala che non torna** to the next design artefacts.

Do not prototype UI yet.

Next required artefacts:

1. World Brief;
2. Role/Agency Map;
3. Information Distribution Map;
4. Consequence Model;
5. Experience Choreography;
6. Visual World Concept.

Only after those pass Human Product Review should a low-fidelity playable prototype be built.

## Why MUSEO ZERO is preferred

It offers the strongest balance of:

- human/social presence without forced intimacy;
- visual world richness;
- meaningful objects and locations;
- genuine investigation;
- visible system consequence;
- natural need to switch representations;
- feasible 2-D mobile implementation;
- enough spontaneity to avoid a teacher-led interaction rhythm.

Most importantly, the learner is not asked to practise “strategy selection”.

The learner is trying to **get a real world working**, and different strategies become useful because the world creates different questions.
