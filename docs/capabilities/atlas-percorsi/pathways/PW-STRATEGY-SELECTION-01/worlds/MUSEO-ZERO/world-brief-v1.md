# MUSEO ZERO — World Brief v1

**Status:** WORLD_BRIEF_CANDIDATE / HUMAN_PRODUCT_REVIEW_REQUIRED  
**Pathway:** PW-STRATEGY-SELECTION-01  
**Runtime:** NOT_RUNTIME_AUTHORIZED  
**World type:** compact 2-D interactive museum / setup environment  
**Target age:** lower secondary / first-cycle learner-facing

## 1. One-sentence world promise

**Domani apre una mostra interattiva. Una sala non funziona più come dovrebbe. Entra nel team di allestimento, scopri che cosa è cambiato e rimetti la sala in condizione di funzionare.**

## 2. Where am I?

Inside **Museo Zero**, a fictional small contemporary museum preparing the opening of a new interactive exhibition.

The experience is concentrated in four connected places:

1. **Ingresso / percorso visitatori**
2. **Sala Zero** — the interactive room with lights, projection and sound
3. **Cabina regia** — where triggers/cues are configured and tested
4. **Laboratorio / deposito** — props, sensor labels, setup notes and physical changes

The learner should perceive these as parts of one place, not as separate pages.

## 3. What was happening before I arrived?

The crew had completed most of the setup.

A late accessibility/space adjustment required reversing part of the visitor route. Several related setup changes were then made quickly:

- visitor entry direction changed;
- a floor sensor was moved to the new entry;
- visual/sound cues were retimed;
- the room was tested again.

During the final rehearsal, the room became unsynchronised:

- visitor enters;
- the intended trigger does not fire at the new entry;
- projection starts late;
- sound and exit lighting fall out of sequence.

No danger is involved. The room is simply not ready for opening.

## 4. Why does the learner matter?

The crew members each saw only part of the setup changes.

The learner joins as a **junior setup operator** who can move between locations, inspect traces, reconstruct what changed, test a model safely in simulation and recommend what to fix.

The learner contributes something the characters currently lack: **a coherent view across places and times**.

## 5. Functional learner role

The learner can:

- inspect locations in chosen order;
- inspect objects/logs/recordings;
- ask crew members about specific moments;
- pin useful evidence to a working surface;
- build a timeline;
- switch to a system/causal view;
- run safe simulated room tests;
- compare recovery options;
- recommend a fix;
- revise after visible failure.

The learner cannot:

- modify real electrical systems;
- access personal data;
- make safety-critical decisions.

## 6. Cast

### Lia — percorso e spazio
Role: exhibition layout / visitor flow.

Carries:
- why the route was changed;
- what the old/new visitor entry is;
- which physical objects were moved.

Does not know the control configuration.

### Omar — installazione interattiva
Role: physical installation / sensors.

Carries:
- which sensor was moved;
- where it was installed;
- what hardware changed.

Does not know the full rehearsal chronology.

### Teo — regia e prove
Role: cue timing / rehearsal observation.

Carries:
- test timestamps;
- what fired late;
- what the control system still expected.

Does not know why the physical route changed.

Characters do not act as tutors. They are participants with partial knowledge.

## 7. What is the actual failure?

### Intended behaviour

At the new visitor entry:

1. visitor crosses **Sensor B**;
2. wall projection starts;
3. 6 seconds later sound cue begins;
4. exit light activates after projection completion.

### Current behaviour

The physical sensor was moved to the new entry, but the active cue remains mapped to **Sensor A / old entry logic**.

During rehearsal:
- visitor enters through new route;
- nothing happens immediately;
- projection starts only after the visitor reaches the old trigger zone;
- audio and exit lighting therefore become visibly late.

## 8. Why multiple strategies become necessary

### Question 1
**“Che cosa è cambiato prima che la sala smettesse di funzionare?”**

Need:
- chronology.

Useful representation:
- timeline.

### Question 2
**“Quale cambiamento ha causato il ritardo dei cue?”**

Need:
- relation between route, sensor, mapping and room behaviour.

Useful representation:
- dependency / causal map.

### Question 3
**“Quale soluzione conviene provare prima dell’apertura?”**

Need:
- compare options against shared constraints.

Useful representation:
- comparison table / constraint view.

The learner experiences why one representation stops being sufficient.

## 9. What can the learner inspect?

### Ingresso
- old/new route arrows;
- accessibility clearance marking;
- moved barrier/prop;
- old sensor marker.

### Sala Zero
- projection wall;
- entry floor sensor;
- light path;
- sound state;
- visitor test path;
- recorded failed rehearsal.

### Cabina regia
- cue chain;
- active trigger mapping;
- test/simulate control;
- timestamped rehearsal log.

### Laboratorio
- sensor labels A/B;
- setup tag showing physical move;
- route-change sketch;
- spare equipment;
- change notes.

## 10. What changes when the learner acts?

- pinning evidence changes the learner’s working model;
- timeline reveals chronology but not cause;
- running a simulation with the wrong trigger reproduces the failure;
- changing the simulated mapping causes the room preview to synchronise;
- choosing a recovery plan changes the rehearsal state;
- crew dialogue changes based on supported findings;
- final room state visibly becomes READY / NEEDS REVISION.

## 11. Productive output

The learner creates:

1. a reconstructed change timeline;
2. a causal/dependency explanation;
3. a supported recovery recommendation.

These are artefacts the crew uses inside the world.

## 12. Curiosity engine

**La sala funzionava. Dopo una serie di modifiche legittime ha smesso di sincronizzarsi. Quale cambiamento conta davvero?**

The learner should initially encounter a visibly strange failed rehearsal, not an instruction panel.

## 13. Emotional register

- curiosity;
- mild time relevance;
- collaborative troubleshooting;
- satisfaction from seeing the room work.

Avoid:
- panic;
- countdown stress;
- blame;
- danger;
- competition.

## 14. Desire-to-continue hypothesis

A plausible 11–14-year-old continuation motive exists if the experience delivers:

- access to a normally hidden backstage/control space;
- a visible malfunction;
- freedom to inspect;
- a genuine unknown cause;
- safe experimentation;
- immediate room response;
- a visible “we fixed it” outcome.

## 15. Failure conditions

Reject the concept if implementation becomes:

- “read Lia card / read Omar card / read Teo card”;
- generic checklist troubleshooting;
- timeline puzzle with museum decoration;
- fixed sequence of required clicks;
- correct/incorrect quiz;
- characters merely restating instructions;
- long technical explanations about sensors/cues.

## 16. Authority boundary

This World Brief authorises no implementation, runtime or Q9 action.
