## 1. Purpose

This pilot proves whether the Factory can convert the already-approved MUSEO ZERO story into a learner-facing visual product that is:
- simple;
- coherent;
- contemporary;
- age-dignified;
- reproducible;
- maintainable;
- strong enough for a real publish/no-publish Human Review.

It is not another engine experiment.

## 2. What the Phaser spike established

The Phaser spike demonstrated:
- a technically working causal mechanism;
- mobile browser execution;
- direct A/B state change;
- a useful negative product result.

It also demonstrated a critical failure mode:
**technical representation can replace the story/world.**

The visible product became sensors, boxes, labels and cables rather than a museum scene experienced by the learner.

The spike is therefore retained as Q1 FUNCTIONAL PROTOTYPE evidence and as an explicit HS05 reference case.

## 3. Pilot product form

The first Factory product will be:

**an illustrated interactive micro-sequence**

Not:
- a full game;
- a full animated film;
- a generic slideshow;
- a technical simulation.

The sequence uses polished keyframes/scene layers with only the motion and interaction needed to make the cause/effect visible.

This deliberately tests visual quality before engine complexity.

## 4. Pilot slice

The first production slice covers:

Beat 1 — failed rehearsal  
through  
Beat 5 — test the explanation and see the room respond correctly.

The later comparison of three fixes remains out of the first visual-factory proof unless the first slice reaches Q3.

## 5. Canonical visual beats

### F1 — After-hours museum
A quiet contemporary museum after closing.
Lia is about to enter Sala Zero.
The learner understands place and mood before seeing any technical representation.

Quality purpose:
establish world credibility.

### F2 — Nothing happens
Lia crosses the new entrance threshold.
The room stays dark/quiet for an awkward beat.

Quality purpose:
make the missing response perceptible without explanation.

### F3 — Too late
Projection/light/sound cue starts after Lia has already moved deeper into the room.
Teo or Omar reacts from the real backstage/regia context.

Quality purpose:
the problem is first experienced, not described.

### F4 — Backstage clue
The learner enters/looks into the control area.
A believable museum control surface exposes the mismatch as an object in the world.

The control may contain labels, but must not become a detached engineering diagram.

Quality purpose:
technical information is diegetic evidence.

### F5 — Learner intervention
The learner changes the relevant mapping/control using the same believable control surface.

Quality purpose:
direct agency.

### F6 — Same rehearsal, correct timing
Lia repeats the same entrance.
The projection/light response now occurs immediately.

Quality purpose:
visible before/after causal proof in the same world.

## 6. Art direction target

Initial direction to test:
- contemporary illustrated realism or restrained graphic-novel realism;
- clean silhouettes;
- controlled palette;
- cinematic but not dark-to-the-point-of-unreadable lighting;
- believable museum architecture;
- restrained backstage technology;
- no cartoon mascot language;
- no pseudo-photorealistic inconsistency;
- no neon cyberpunk default;
- no generic "AI game concept art" look.

The pilot must compare at least two style frames before locking direction.

## 7. Character pack

Required:
- Lia canonical reference;
- Omar canonical reference;
- Teo canonical reference;
- one neutral group/world scale reference.

Before scene production, Human Review must confirm:
- age appropriateness;
- identity continuity;
- no stereotyped visual shorthand;
- compatibility with the museum world;
- expressions readable without caricature.

## 8. Environment pack

Required:
- Sala Zero master view;
- new entrance;
- projection wall;
- exit light;
- backstage/control booth;
- route floor treatment;
- limited recurring props.

The environment should make the learner feel "backstage at a museum", not "inside a software dashboard".

## 9. Factory sequence

### P0 — Production pack
Complete the Visual Production Pack template for F1–F6.

### P1 — Style-frame sprint
Generate/refine a bounded set of style frames.

Maximum objective:
enough variants to compare directions, not dozens of random images.

Human decision:
ART_DIRECTION_SELECTED or RETURN.

### P2 — Reference lock
Lock:

- environment master;
- palette/light;
- composition language.

No animation before this lock.

### P3 — Keyframe production
Produce F1–F6 as polished still keyframes/layers.

Run:
- five-frame continuity;
- AI artefact sweep;
- age-dignity review;
- first-frame credibility.

Required:
D1–D4 at Q2 or higher before motion/interaction.

### P4 — Interactive composition
Add the minimum interaction needed for F4/F5 and the before/after replay.

Godot is the leading candidate for this pilot, but its use remains conditional on export/deployment fit.
A lighter web composition is acceptable if it preserves the reviewed product and does not recreate the Phaser/debug-screen failure.

### P5 — Optional motion
Only add motion that carries:
- Lia entering;
- delayed response;
- corrected response;
- small character reaction;
- transition into backstage clue.

No decorative ambient animation requirement.

### P6 — Human Quality Review
Apply VISUAL-QUALITY-BAR-v0.1 on real phone and desktop.

Target:
Q3 PUBLISHABLE CRAFT.

### P7 — Decision
- PUBLISH_CANDIDATE;

## 10. Pilot hard constraints

Reject or return if:
- the scene is primarily boxes/arrows/panels;
- generative character identity drifts;
- the museum world feels like generic concept art with no continuity;
- technical labels carry the story;
- the learner action happens in a detached HTML form;
- mobile requires repeated scrolling between instruction and action;
- video generation is used to hide weak keyframes;
- the production recipe cannot be reproduced;
- the style feels infantilising for lower secondary.

## 11. Success evidence

A successful pilot stores:
- selected/rejected style frames;
- canonical character references;
- canonical environment reference;
- F1–F6 keyframes;
- workflow/recipe references;
- editable masters where practical;
- exact candidate build;
- phone screenshots;
- Q3 review;
- final Human Product decision.

## 12. Tool-cost experiment

The pilot will measure:
- number of generation/refinement iterations;
- human refinement time;
- compute dependency;
- asset reuse ratio;
- build/export complexity;
- defects per accepted frame;
- whether motion/video adds enough value to keep.

This evidence decides the v0.2 toolchain.
