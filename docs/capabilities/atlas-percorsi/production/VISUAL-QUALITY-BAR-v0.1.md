# TRAMA Visual Quality Bar v0.1

**Status:** PROPOSED_HUMAN_QUALITY_GATE  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED  
**Target:** lower-secondary and first-cycle learner-facing visual products  
**Factory target:** Q3 PUBLISHABLE CRAFT

## 1. Quality statement

The minimum acceptable learner-facing quality is:

**simple but deliberately made.**

A product does not need cinematic assets, photorealism, complex 3D or continuous animation.

It does need to look like a finished product rather than:
- a wireframe;
- a debug tool;
- a generic AI mockup;
- a school worksheet moved onto a dark background;
- a technical diagram presented as a world;
- an inconsistent collection of generated images.

The quality bar protects learner dignity and product credibility.

## 2. Why the bar is high

Young learners encounter polished digital products, games, video and social media in everyday life, although usage patterns differ widely.

TRAMA must not compete by increasing stimulation.
It must compete on:
- clarity;
- craft;
- coherence;
- curiosity;
- agency;
- consequence;
- visual confidence.

The learner should not have to lower expectations because the product is educational.

## 3. Quality levels

### Q0 — STRUCTURAL SKETCH
Purpose:
- think;
- communicate layout;
- prove a story beat.

Characteristics:
- placeholders allowed;
- inconsistent style allowed;
- wireframes allowed;
- debug labels allowed.

Learner-facing publication:
PROHIBITED.

### Q1 — FUNCTIONAL PROTOTYPE
Purpose:
- prove mechanics or technical feasibility.

Characteristics:
- real interaction may work;
- visuals may be provisional;
- art direction may be incomplete;
- obvious prototype residue may remain.

Learner-facing publication:
PROHIBITED.

The MUSEO ZERO Phaser sensor/cable spike is the reference example of a technically qualified Q1 that fails as a product.

### Q2 — REVIEWABLE PRODUCT CANDIDATE
Purpose:
- serious Human Product Review.

Characteristics:
- coherent visual direction;
- no major placeholders;
- stable characters/world;
- meaningful interaction visible;
- mobile composition reviewable;
- remaining defects are identifiable and bounded.

Learner-facing publication:
NOT YET DEFAULT.

### Q3 — PUBLISHABLE CRAFT
Purpose:
- candidate for formal publish/no-publish decision.

Characteristics:
- no visible prototype residue;
- no obvious generative defects;
- coherent art direction across the complete slice;
- stable characters and world;
- clear learner action;
- consequences are visible in the same world;
- age tone is respectful;
- mobile surface feels composed rather than compressed;
- accessibility alternatives preserve meaning;
- asset provenance and production recipe are governed.

This is the required Visual Factory v0.1 target.

### Q4 — SHOWCASE / PREMIUM
Purpose:
- exceptional flagship work.

Characteristics may include:
- richer animation;
- sophisticated sound;
- advanced environmental storytelling;
- unusually high asset polish.

Q4 is welcome but never required for ordinary publication.

## 4. Hard stops

Any one of these blocks PUBLISH_CANDIDATE regardless of score.

### HS01 — Prototype residue
Visible:
- debug labels;
- developer terminology;
- wireframe geometry;
- temporary boxes/panels;
- obvious placeholder assets.

### HS02 — Generic AI look
The scene looks like unrelated generated images assembled together rather than one authored world.

### HS03 — Character identity instability
A recurring character materially changes face, clothing language, proportions, age appearance or defining visual identity without narrative reason.

### HS04 — Obvious generative artefacts
Examples:
- malformed hands/objects;
- impossible geometry that distracts;
- broken signage/text;
- duplicated/merged objects;
- unexplained visual mutations.

Minor painterly stylisation is not a defect when deliberate and consistent.

### HS05 — Technical diagram replacing the world
The core experience is explained through labels, boxes, arrows, cables, state panels or system notation when the approved story requires a lived place/situation.

Technical representations are allowed when they are themselves the authentic learning object and are staged appropriately.

### HS06 — Learner action detached from representation
The learner reads one thing and answers/acts in a separate generic control surface when direct action in the represented situation is feasible.

### HS07 — Infantilising lower-secondary tone
Mascot treatment, language, reward effects or visual simplification materially underestimates the target age band.

### HS08 — Mobile composition failure
Essential instruction, material and action are separated by excessive scrolling, cropped, unreadable or obscured on the target phone viewport.

### HS09 — Unclear provenance/usage basis
A material asset has no acceptable source/usage/provenance record.

### HS10 — Unrecoverable one-off production
The team cannot reasonably revise or replace accepted assets because the production process, editable master or workflow reference has been lost.

## 5. Eight review dimensions

Score each dimension from 0 to 3:
- 0 = unfit/broken;
- 1 = prototype;
- 2 = serious review candidate;
- 3 = publishable craft.

The number supports disciplined discussion. Human Review remains authority.

### D1 — Story/world fidelity
Does the visual product actually stage the approved story/world rather than merely decorate a lesson?

Q3 indicators:
- setting is perceptible;
- characters have believable presence;
- story beat is visible;
- learner role makes sense;
- removing the story would change the task.

### D2 — Art direction and continuity
Does the product feel authored as one visual system?

Q3 indicators:
- coherent palette/light/material language;
- stable composition grammar;
- stable recurring anchors;
- consistent character/world identity;
- no scene-to-scene style lottery.

### D3 — Asset finish
Do visible assets look intentional at normal learner viewing size?

Q3 indicators:
- no obvious generation artefacts;
- clean silhouettes/edges where relevant;
- readable diegetic text;
- appropriate detail density;
- no unexplained distortions.

### D4 — Learner clarity
Can the learner understand what is happening and what to do without teacher translation?

Q3 indicators:
- first meaningful frame has a clear focal point;
- situation/action become legible quickly;
- instructions do not compete with the scene;
- state change is visible;
- recovery after hesitation is possible.

### D5 — Agency and consequence
Does learner action change something meaningful?

Q3 indicators:
- learner acts on the relevant representation;
- action has a visible consequence;
- consequence supports reasoning;
- interaction is not decorative tapping.

### D6 — Age dignity
Does the product respect the target age?

Q3 indicators:
- no babyish visual shorthand by default;
- characters are not fake friends or cheerleaders;
- humour, if any, is situational rather than patronising;
- challenge and ambiguity are allowed when appropriate.

### D7 — Mobile/accessibility composition
Does the experience remain coherent on the real delivery surface?

Q3 indicators:
- reviewed at 390x844 or equivalent common phone viewport;
- no horizontal overflow;
- touch targets are comfortably operable;
- focus state is visible;
- reduced-motion/no-audio alternatives preserve meaning;
- essential task elements remain perceptually connected.

Internal ergonomic target:
prefer touch targets around 44x44 CSS px or larger where the composition allows.

### D8 — Production integrity
Can the asset/product be maintained responsibly?

Q3 indicators:
- canonical asset identified;
- source/provenance known;
- editable master or replacement recipe available;
- dependency/toolchain recorded;
- final derivatives separated from masters;
- no hidden runtime tracking.

## 6. Q3 promotion rule

A candidate may be proposed as PUBLISH_CANDIDATE only when:
- no hard stop is open;
- all eight dimensions score at least 2;
- D2 Art direction, D3 Asset finish, D4 Learner clarity and D6 Age dignity score 3;
- total score is at least 21/24;
- Human Review explicitly records PUBLISH_CANDIDATE.

A numeric pass never forces publication.
Human Review may reject or return a candidate even when the structural threshold is met.

Any waiver below the threshold must be documented and cannot waive child-safety, accessibility, provenance or runtime rules.

## 7. Practical review tests

### T1 — First-frame credibility
Take a screenshot with no explanation.

Question:
Does this look like a purposeful learner-facing product, or like a prototype/debug/admin screen?

Prototype/debug impression = fail for Q3.

### T2 — Ten-second comprehension
A reviewer unfamiliar with the build answers:
- what is happening?;
- what seems important?;
- what can I do next?

If verbal explanation is required, D4 cannot score 3.

### T3 — Five-frame continuity
Compare five representative frames from the slice.

Check:
- same characters?;
- same world?;
- same art direction?;
- same visual anchors?;
- state changes understandable?

### T4 — AI artefact sweep
Inspect the actual learner viewing size and a zoomed QA view.

Reject defects that:
- attract attention unintentionally;
- change meaning;
- break character identity;
- make the world feel generated rather than authored.

### T5 — No-instruction screenshot
Temporarily hide explanatory prose.

Question:
Does the scene still communicate place, actors and current state?

If not, the prose may be carrying the experience.

### T6 — Phone-in-hand review
Use a real phone where possible.

Check:
- thumb reach;
- reading size;
- scroll burden;
- focus;
- overlays;
- relation between instruction/material/action.

### T7 — Consequence test
Perform the key learner action.

Question:
Can the reviewer see what changed without reading a separate result card?

### T8 — Rebuild/revision test
Ask another operator to locate:
- canonical source assets;
- workflow/recipe;
- editable master;
- quality decision.

If this depends on chat memory, D8 cannot score 3.

## 8. Visual style policy

No single style is canonical.

Permitted families include:
- illustrated realism;
- graphic novel / sequential art;
- restrained stylised 2D;
- cut-out/motion illustration;
- diagrammatic visualisation when genuinely appropriate;
- compact 2D game-world representation.

The chosen style must satisfy:
- story fit;
- production repeatability;
- age dignity;
- readability;
- asset continuity;
- affordable maintenance.

Photorealism is not inherently higher quality.
A strong simplified illustration can score Q3 more easily than unstable pseudo-realistic output.

## 9. Motion and video policy

Motion must carry:
- causality;
- time;
- attention cue;
- character action;
- transition;
- consequence.

Decorative motion alone does not raise quality.

AI-generated video is optional and cannot rescue weak art direction.
Before video generation, canonical stills should already pass D1–D4 at Q2 or better.

## 10. Sound policy

Sound is optional.

If used:
- no autoplay dependency for task comprehension;
- dialogue/information has text alternative;
- volume is controllable;
- no reward sounds designed primarily for compulsive return;
- silence may be part of the atmosphere.

## 11. Current benchmark posture

Minecraft Education is a useful mature-product contrast because it treats learning as activity inside coherent worlds rather than as an excuse for low visual craft.

TRAMA does not need Minecraft-scale production.
The lesson is narrower:
**the educational purpose does not excuse a visibly unfinished world.**

Current youth media research also reinforces that young people's digital lives span games, video, social media and other forms rather than one uniform "average" medium. Therefore the Factory must not design for a caricature of "today's student" or simply copy short-form-video stimulation.

## 12. Human Review record

Each review records:
- candidate ID;
- exact source revision;
- target age;
- device/viewport reviewed;
- hard stops;
- D1–D8 scores with short rationale;
- screenshots/evidence references;
- decision;
- required remediation;
- reviewer/date.
