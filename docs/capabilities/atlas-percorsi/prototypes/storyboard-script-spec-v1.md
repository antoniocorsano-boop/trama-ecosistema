# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — Controlled Script & Storyboard Specification v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery  
**Status:** CONCEPT_SCRIPT / NOT_USER_VALIDATED  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED  
**Depends on:** `metaphor-controlled-comparison-v1.md`, `emotional-development-safeguards.md`, `research-reasoning-emotion-learning.md`

## Purpose

Translate the controlled metaphor comparison into a script/storyboard specification without yet committing to polished graphics, animation libraries, characters, sound design or a final metaphor.

The storyboard exists to make pedagogical, cognitive, emotional, accessibility, privacy and sustainability assumptions inspectable before implementation.

## Controlled scene: shared decision

The same fictional situation is retained across variants and age bands: a group must decide how to continue a shared task when one perspective has not yet been heard.

### Pedagogical invariant

The learner should have an opportunity to:

`ORIENT → NOTICE → CHOOSE → OBSERVE CONSEQUENCE → RECONSIDER → TRANSFER`

No scene may infer a personality trait or emotional state from the learner's choice.

---

# Storyboard grammar

Every storyboard frame must declare:

- **Frame ID**;
- **pedagogical function**;
- **literal learner instruction**;
- **dominant attentional focus**;
- **stable visual anchors** inherited from the previous frame;
- **new/changed element and why it changes**;
- **learner action**;
- **feedback/consequence**;
- **emotion role**;
- **privacy/data state**;
- **accessibility/low-stimulation equivalent**;
- **motion/audio necessity**;
- **resource/sustainability note**;
- **failure signal**.

A frame that cannot explain why a visual, motion or audio element exists should remove it.

# Sequence S0 — Entry and orientation

## S0-F1 — What is happening?

**Pedagogical function:** establish context without requiring inference from imagery.  
**Literal instruction:** age-adjusted sentence explaining that a group must decide how to continue and that not everyone has contributed yet.  
**Dominant focus:** shared task and group.  
**Stable anchors:** group members, shared object/task, fixed spatial arrangement.  
**New element:** none; this is the baseline.  
**Learner action:** continue when ready.  
**Emotion role:** mild social uncertainty may be visible in the fictional situation; no dramatic distress.  
**Privacy/data:** no learner input stored.  
**Accessibility:** text remains sufficient; illustration is supportive.  
**Motion/audio:** none required.  
**Sustainability:** one reusable scene composition.  
**Failure signal:** learner must inspect facial expressions to understand the task.

## S0-F2 — Notice the missing perspective

**Pedagogical function:** direct attention to the fact that one perspective has not entered the decision.  
**Literal instruction:** e.g. "One person has not shared an idea yet."  
**Dominant focus:** the missing contribution, not the child's inferred emotion.  
**Stable anchors:** all S0-F1 elements remain in place.  
**Changed element:** a neutral contribution marker remains empty/absent.  
**Learner action:** acknowledge/continue.  
**Emotion role:** no label such as sad, shy or anxious.  
**Privacy/data:** none.  
**Accessibility:** absence is expressed in text and structure, not colour alone.  
**Motion/audio:** none required.  
**Failure signal:** scene implies a psychological explanation for why the peer has not spoken.

---

# Sequence S1 — Choice

## S1-F1 — Strategy options

**Pedagogical function:** present bounded meaningful choice.  
**Literal instruction:** "How could the group decide what to do next?" with age-adjusted wording.  
**Dominant focus:** strategy options.  
**Stable anchors:** group/task remain visible but visually secondary.  
**Learner action:** select one strategy.  
**Feedback:** no immediate praise/reward.  
**Emotion role:** neutral decision space.  
**Privacy/data:** choice may exist only in ephemeral/local session state; no identity binding.  
**Accessibility:** keyboard/touch accessible; explicit text labels; no colour-only distinction.  
**Motion/audio:** none required.  
**Failure signal:** visual styling reveals which answer designers prefer.

### Literal variant

Strategies are plain cards/buttons.

### Journey/Crossroads variant

Strategies are placed at a restrained junction, but literal labels remain primary. Paths have equal visual salience; no gold/bright/short "correct" path.

### Workshop/Tools variant

Strategies appear as simple labelled tools/cards. Tools are not collectible, unlockable or rarity-coded.

---

# Sequence S2 — Consequence

## S2-F1 — What changed?

**Pedagogical function:** make the consequence of the selected process visible without moralising.  
**Dominant focus:** difference in participation/information available to the group.  
**Stable anchors:** preserve group positions, shared task and core layout.  
**Changed element:** only the contribution/process outcome needed to understand the consequence.  
**Learner action:** inspect, then continue.  
**Feedback:** descriptive rather than evaluative: "The group decided quickly, but one idea was not heard" / "The group heard another idea before deciding."  
**Emotion role:** no shame, celebration or exaggerated reaction.  
**Privacy/data:** none beyond ephemeral branch state.  
**Accessibility:** consequence described in text; visual change optional reinforcement.  
**Motion/audio:** a short transition may be considered only if it clarifies causality; static equivalent must be complete.  
**Failure signal:** learner mainly remembers an animation/reaction rather than the consequence relation.

---

# Sequence S3 — Reconsideration

## S3-F1 — Keep or revise

**Pedagogical function:** normalise revision as thinking.  
**Literal instruction:** "Now that you have seen what happened, would you keep this choice or try another way?"  
**Dominant focus:** revision opportunity.  
**Stable anchors:** consequence remains available for reference.  
**Learner action:** keep/revise.  
**Feedback:** both actions permitted; no loss of points/progress.  
**Emotion role:** reduce fear of being wrong.  
**Privacy/data:** local ephemeral choice only.  
**Accessibility:** consequence can be reopened/re-read.  
**Motion/audio:** none required.  
**Failure signal:** interface visually frames revision as backtracking/failure.

---

# Sequence S4 — Reasoning

## S4-F1 — Why might that strategy fit?

**Pedagogical function:** require processing beyond visual recall.  
**Learner action:** choose/arrange an age-appropriate reason; optional local note only for older learners if later governed.  
**Dominant focus:** relation between strategy and consequence.  
**Stable anchors:** minimal reference to previous scene.  
**Feedback:** explanatory, not personality-based.  
**Emotion role:** none required.  
**Privacy/data:** no required free text; any future optional note remains local by default.  
**Accessibility:** plain language and readable structure.  
**Failure signal:** task can be solved by remembering icon placement rather than understanding the relation.

---

# Sequence S5 — Transfer

## S5-F1 — A changed situation

**Pedagogical function:** test whether the learner can use the principle beyond the first scene.  
**Scene:** same underlying issue, different surface context (e.g. allocating materials, assigning roles, evaluating competing source claims for older learners).  
**Dominant focus:** relevant decision information.  
**Stable anchors:** retain only the minimal metaphor scaffold if used; do not reuse the original image as a cue to the answer.  
**Learner action:** select an appropriate process and reason.  
**Feedback:** connects action to context, not to a trait score.  
**Emotion role:** ordinary social/cognitive tension only.  
**Privacy/data:** no autobiographical disclosure.  
**Accessibility:** literal statement sufficient.  
**Failure signal:** learner succeeds only when the same icons/road/tool layout is repeated.

---

# Sequence S6 — Trace / learner-controlled artefact

## S6-F1 — What can be kept?

**Pedagogical function:** preserve a reusable strategy insight without creating a student profile.  
**Candidate output:** a small local "strategy card" or journey/tool note such as: "Before a shared decision, check whose information is still missing."  
**Learner action:** keep locally, export/download later if that capability is governed, or discard.  
**Privacy/data:** no name required; no automatic upload; minimal metadata.  
**Accessibility:** printable/text equivalent.  
**Sustainability:** generate a compact artefact rather than a media-heavy certificate.  
**Failure signal:** artefact becomes a badge, status symbol, personality label or public-share prompt.

# Age-band adaptation contract

## Early primary

- one dominant decision at a time;
- two primary options unless the pedagogical reason for more is explicit;
- short literal sentences;
- stable characters/layout;
- concrete consequences;
- no abstract trait vocabulary;
- transfer remains close enough to recognise the relation but not visually identical.

## Later primary

- up to three bounded strategies when justified;
- explicit reason selection;
- revision after consequence;
- more complex but still concrete trade-offs;
- metaphor can support orientation but cannot carry the instruction.

## Lower secondary

- realistic trade-offs rather than moral binaries;
- greater information density only when necessary;
- opportunity to weigh time, participation, evidence and consequences;
- visual language must avoid childish fantasy/game cues;
- optional local written reasoning may be considered later, never required for public use;
- transfer can cross from social collaboration to information/evidence decisions.

---

# Emotional safeguard contract

The storyboard must never:

- demand disclosure of the learner's private emotional experience;
- infer emotion from choice, timing, text or behaviour;
- label a learner empathetic/unempathetic, anxious/calm, collaborative/non-collaborative;
- use humiliation, guilt, fear, urgency or peer comparison as engagement mechanics;
- punish skipping, pausing or revising;
- simulate a therapeutic or diagnostic interaction.

When an emotionally sensitive theme exceeds ordinary educational reflection, the activity must be reclassified for adult/teacher mediation before publication.

---

# Visual attention contract

For each frame:

1. one dominant pedagogical focus;
2. stable anchors persist unless change itself is meaningful;
3. novelty must carry new educational information;
4. no automatic carousel or scene churn;
5. no decorative motion adjacent to the reasoning target;
6. the learner controls progression through essential content;
7. return/re-read is available;
8. imagery supports but does not replace literal meaning;
9. colour is never the sole carrier of meaning;
10. low-stimulation/reduced-motion equivalence is designed simultaneously.

---

# Data and privacy contract

Conceptual data inventory for this storyboard:

| Action | Data needed | Location | Server transmission | Retention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| open scene | none personal | client/session | none required | none |
| choose strategy | choice state | ephemeral/local | none required | session/local only |
| revise choice | choice state | ephemeral/local | none required | session/local only |
| select reason | response state | ephemeral/local | none required | session/local only |
| transfer task | response state | ephemeral/local | none required | session/local only |
| keep strategy trace | generated non-identifying artefact | local device | none by default | learner-controlled |
| export/download | artefact bytes | local device | no Atlas upload | learner-controlled |

No analytics requirement is introduced by this storyboard. Any later proposal for measurement must separately demonstrate necessity and must not create student tracking.

# Accessibility review questions

Before visual production:

- Is the task fully understandable from text/structure alone?
- Can all actions be performed without precision dragging?
- Is focus order predictable?
- Are touch targets adequate?
- Does zoom/reflow preserve the scene meaning?
- Is there a reduced-motion/no-audio equivalent?
- Are icons labelled rather than assumed to be universally understood?
- Can a learner recover context after interruption?
- Is reading load appropriate to the age band?
- Are consequences expressed without relying on facial-expression interpretation?

---

# Sustainability/resource budget questions

Before choosing technical libraries/assets:

- Can the scene reuse a stable composition rather than load a new full illustration for every step?
- Is animation necessary to explain causality or merely decorative?
- Can vector/simple raster assets achieve the same pedagogical function as video/3D?
- Can essential assets be cached/offline-capable?
- Are third-party network calls necessary?
- Can exported artefacts remain compact and text-first?
- Does the proposed atmosphere add educational meaning proportional to its computational/network cost?

No CO2e/energy/water quantity should be inferred from these questions without a documented methodology.

---

# Review matrix before any polished prototype

A storyboard candidate is ready for the next design experiment only if reviewers can answer **yes** to all mandatory conditions:

| Domain | Mandatory condition |
|---|---|
| Pedagogy | objective, action, consequence, reflection and transfer are explicit |
| Child safety | no diagnosis, profiling, coercion or unnecessary disclosure |
| Emotion | significance serves learning rather than attention capture |
| Cognitive load | dominant focus and stable anchors are defined |
| Metaphor | literal comprehension does not depend on metaphor |
| Accessibility | equivalent meaning exists without motion/audio/figurative decoding |
| Privacy | meaningful use requires no personal account/profile/server reflection |
| Agency | pause, revise and recover are non-punitive |
| Sustainability | unnecessary media/requests/computation are removed before optimisation |
| Governance | no runtime or cross-product authority is inferred from the storyboard |
