# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — G1 Review: Journey Multi-Scene Prototype v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery  
**Status:** INTERNAL_REVIEW / NO_METAPHOR_SELECTION  
**Reviewed prototype:** `journey-multiscene-prototype-v1.md`  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED

## Review purpose

Determine whether the current Journey/Crossroads multi-scene hypothesis is coherent enough to justify a controlled visual storyboard, while identifying what must be corrected or evidenced before any metaphor selection.

## Review result

**PROVISIONAL PASS FOR LOW-FIDELITY STORYBOARD ONLY.**

This is not a G1 closure, not a product-design approval and not permission to implement runtime.

## Strengths retained

### 1. Context is doing pedagogical work

The journey is no longer merely an illustration around choices. A stable place can preserve causal continuity between situation, choice, consequence, reconsideration and later retrieval.

### 2. Reconsideration is structurally legitimate

Returning to a junction can make revision visible without framing it as failure, provided the interface avoids loss/progress metaphors.

### 3. Strategy extraction occurs after experience

The strategy/tool appears only after the learner has encountered a situation and consequence. This reduces the risk that the tool becomes a collectible or decontextualised rule.

### 4. Transfer is explicitly separated from repetition

The prototype changes the surface context and then the domain of the decision, reducing the chance that success is caused only by repeating the same icon or route.

### 5. Privacy remains compatible with the experience

The conceptual sequence requires no account, personal profile, autobiographical disclosure or server-side learner trace.

## Risks requiring active control

### R1 — Journey may imply one correct life path

**Risk:** path imagery can carry a moral meaning stronger than the text.

**Control:** equal visual status for alternatives; consequence/trade-off language; no destination framed as personal success; no permanent learner path identity.

### R2 — Context may become scenery

**Risk:** the desire for atmosphere may cause visual world-building that competes with the competence relation.

**Control:** every environmental element requires a pedagogical/orientation function; stable background; selective change; visual budget review.

### R3 — Continuity may become narrative dependency

**Risk:** learners may remember the story but fail to retrieve the strategy outside it.

**Control:** literal naming, changed-context retrieval and cross-domain transfer are mandatory.

### R4 — Emotional coding may leak through characters

**Risk:** facial expressions and body language can imply sadness, exclusion, guilt or moral judgement not present in the learning objective.

**Control:** restrained expressions; state the missing information explicitly; do not require emotion-reading to solve the task.

### R5 — Tool representation may become gamification

**Risk:** strategy cards/tools can become rewards, collections, unlocks or progress currency.

**Control:** no inventory economy; no rarity; no badges; no reward animation; learner may keep/discard a compact local trace.

### R6 — Age scaling may fail

**Risk:** the same journey aesthetic can be appropriate in primary and infantilising in lower secondary.

**Control:** preserve narrative architecture but mature the representation from concrete place to restrained orientation/decision map.

### R7 — Visual continuity can hide excessive cognitive density

**Risk:** keeping one scene stable does not automatically make it simple; too many persistent elements can themselves burden attention.

**Control:** one dominant pedagogical focus; remove anchors that no longer support orientation; do not equate persistence with necessity.

## Required storyboard controls

The next low-fidelity storyboard must show, for each frame:

- exact literal instruction;
- dominant focus;
- stable anchors;
- single meaningful visual change where feasible;
- learner action;
- consequence/feedback;
- low-stimulation equivalent;
- data state;
- reason for any motion;
- reason for every contextual object retained.

## Visual production rule

The next prototype must be **wireframe/structural first**. Do not generate a polished fantasy world.

For later-primary comparison:

- use the same group, task and spatial arrangement across Scenes 1–6;
- keep route geometry neutral;
- use labels as the primary carrier of strategy meaning;
- represent the strategy tool only after Scene 5;
- introduce a visibly changed context only at transfer;
- avoid expressive character reactions;
- avoid decorative animation and automatic transitions;
- design reduced-motion/low-stimulation equivalence simultaneously.

## Evidence still missing before metaphor selection

G1 still needs to establish, through appropriate later validation rather than assumption:

1. whether contextual continuity actually improves orientation;
2. whether learners retain the causal/strategic relation rather than mainly the setting;
3. whether the strategy can be retrieved without the original journey cue;
4. whether route presentation creates unintended answer bias;
5. whether revision is perceived as legitimate rather than failure;
6. whether the architecture remains age-respectful across bands;
7. whether literal presentation performs equally well with lower cognitive/resource cost;
8. whether the architecture generalises across the planned transversal competence territories.

## G1 decision

The Journey/Crossroads hypothesis is sufficiently coherent to justify a **controlled low-fidelity storyboard for later primary**.

It is **not yet sufficiently evidenced to become the canonical Atlas Percorsi metaphor**.

The literal baseline remains mandatory. Workshop/Tools remains a subordinate candidate representation for reusable strategies, not a second narrative world.
