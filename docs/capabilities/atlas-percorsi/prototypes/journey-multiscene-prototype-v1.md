# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — Journey Multi-Scene Prototype v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery  
**Status:** CONCEPT_PROTOTYPE / LEADING_HYPOTHESIS TEST  
**Age band under test:** later primary (approx. 9–10 years; not a diagnostic category)  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED

## Test question

Does a restrained Journey/Crossroads architecture add useful **contextual continuity** to the same pedagogical sequence without increasing cognitive load, emotional pressure, visual replacement or metaphor dependency?

This prototype tests continuity, not preference and not visual appeal.

## Pedagogical invariant

Fictional situation: a small group must decide how to continue a shared classroom task when one perspective has not yet been heard.

Learning sequence:

`ORIENT → NOTICE → CHOOSE → CONSEQUENCE → RECONSIDER → RECOGNISE STRATEGY → CHANGED CONTEXT → TRANSFER → KEEP/DISCARD TRACE`

No step assesses personality, empathy, emotional type or moral worth.

---

# Scene 1 — The shared place

## Narrative function

Establish one coherent place that will remain recognisable through the first decision cycle.

## Literal layer

"The group is working together. They need to decide how to continue. One person has not shared an idea yet."

## Journey layer

The group is represented at a simple orientation point with the shared task visibly present. A path continues ahead but reaches a junction. The junction does not yet display dramatic alternatives.

## Stable anchors

- same group positions;
- same shared-task object;
- same orientation marker;
- same restrained background/landmark.

## Learner action

Observe and continue when ready.

## Prohibited additions

No expressive sadness/shyness coding; no mascot; no dramatic music; no moving scenery; no reward indicator.

---

# Scene 2 — The missing contribution

## Narrative function

Make the relevant information visible without assigning an emotion or motive.

## Literal layer

"Three ideas are on the table. One person's idea is still missing."

## Journey layer

The same orientation point remains. Four contribution markers belong to the shared task; three contain an idea and one remains empty. The path/junction is still secondary.

## Cognitive action

The learner identifies what information is missing.

## Continuity test

Nothing changes except the pedagogically relevant contribution marker.

---

# Scene 3 — The crossroads

## Narrative function

Represent a bounded decision.

## Literal choices

A. Decide now with the ideas already available.  
B. Hear one idea from each person, then decide.  
C. Let the person who proposed the task decide.

## Journey representation

Three equally weighted route options appear at the same junction. Literal text is attached to each route and remains primary.

## Visual controls

- same width/length at the decision point;
- no bright/dark moral coding;
- no route placed as the obvious centre/default;
- no treasure, finish flag or reward symbol;
- no animation revealing a preferred route.

## Learner action

Choose one process.

---

# Scene 4 — Consequence without teleportation

## Narrative function

Show what changed while preserving place and orientation.

## Key design rule

**Do not replace the scene with a new illustration.**

The learner advances only a small distance along the selected route. The original junction remains retrievable/partly visible. The shared task and group remain stable.

## Consequence examples

- immediate decision: less time used, but one contribution remains absent;
- one idea each: more information becomes available, with a small time cost;
- proposer decides: responsibility is concentrated, while other contributions remain unused.

No branch is celebrated or punished. The consequence is contextual and may involve trade-offs.

## Learner action

Identify what changed.

---

# Scene 5 — Return is thinking

## Narrative function

Make reconsideration legitimate.

## Literal prompt

"Now that you have seen what happened, would you keep this way or try another?"

## Journey representation

The junction remains reachable. Returning is represented as revisiting a decision point, not losing progress.

## Prohibited mechanics

- no lost points;
- no broken streak;
- no red failure state;
- no shame language;
- no animation of going backwards as defeat.

## Learner action

Keep or revise.

---

# Scene 6 — Name the reusable strategy

## Narrative function

Extract a transferable idea from the context.

## Candidate strategy

"Before a shared decision, check whose useful information is still missing."

## Workshop/Tools integration

Here, and only after experience, a restrained **strategy tool/card** may appear. It is not a prize. It names what the learner has just used/observed.

## Rule

The strategy exists independently of the icon. Text/literal meaning remains complete.

## Learner action

Match the strategy to the consequence it helps address.

---

# Scene 7 — Continue the journey, change the surface context

## Narrative function

Test retrieval in a related but non-identical situation.

## New fictional situation

The group now has limited materials and must decide how to distribute them before beginning a different task. One relevant piece of information about needs has not yet been gathered.

## Continuity

A few stable orientation elements remain, but characters/objects do not reproduce the answer cue from the first scene.

## Learner action

Decide whether the earlier strategy is useful here and choose an action.

## Critical test

Success should require recognising the underlying relation, not selecting the same road/icon position.

---

# Scene 8 — Transfer beyond the social surface

## Narrative function

Test whether the strategy generalises beyond "let everyone speak".

## Situation

The learner sees two information sources for a simple class inquiry. Before choosing which to use, one important piece of information about a source is missing.

## Prompt

"What should you check before deciding?"

## Purpose

The earlier principle becomes broader: **identify relevant missing information before a consequential choice**.

This prevents the pathway from teaching only a scripted social response.

---

# Scene 9 — Learner-controlled trace

## Narrative function

Allow the insight to persist without creating a profile.

## Candidate local trace

A compact strategy card:

> Before I decide, I can check what important information is still missing.

No name, score, badge, level or emotional label.

## Learner controls

- keep locally;
- discard;
- future download/export only if governed separately.

No automatic server upload.

---

# Literal baseline

The same nine functions must also exist without Journey imagery:

1. context card;
2. missing-information marker;
3. three text choices;
4. consequence card;
5. keep/revise action;
6. strategy statement;
7. changed situation;
8. transfer situation;
9. local strategy trace.

The Journey candidate only survives if it adds orientation/contextual continuity without reducing clarity or transfer relative to this baseline.

---

# Attention and image-continuity budget

Across Scenes 1–6:

- one stable base composition;
- no more than one pedagogically meaningful visual change per step where feasible;
- no automatic transition;
- no decorative character animation;
- no full-background replacement;
- consequence changes are local and inspectable;
- previous state can be revisited;
- literal text remains available;
- no visual novelty solely to "refresh" attention.

Scenes 7–8 may introduce a changed context because transfer requires it, but should retain a restrained orientation grammar rather than a cinematic new world.

---

# Emotional safety review

The prototype deliberately uses ordinary social uncertainty rather than emotionally intense conflict.

It must not:

- imply that the unheard fictional peer is sad, anxious or rejected unless that state is pedagogically necessary and explicitly governed;
- ask the learner to remember a personal exclusion/conflict;
- reward conformity;
- equate consultation with being a "good person";
- shame fast decision-making;
- present one strategy as universally correct regardless of context.

The educational target is reasoning about information, participation and consequences.

---

# Context-coherence test

After each scene, the design team should be able to answer:

- What stayed the same?
- What changed?
- Why did it change?
- What does the learner need to remember from before?
- Does the next scene require that memory?
- Is the visual change proportional to the new information?

If the answer to the last question is no, reduce the visual change.

---

# Candidate validation protocol — no covert profiling

A later human validation, if authorised, should use aggregate/design-level observations rather than persistent individual learner profiles.

Questions include:

- Can learners explain the task without referring to the road metaphor?
- Can they say what changed after the choice?
- Do they understand that returning/revising is allowed?
- Can they retrieve the strategy in Scene 7?
- Can they generalise it in Scene 8?
- Do they mistake one route's visual appearance for correctness?
- Which visual elements do they recall, and do they also recall the underlying relation?
- Does the literal baseline produce equal or better understanding with less burden?

No psychological inference should be drawn from an individual learner's path choice.

---

# Decision rule

Journey/Crossroads should advance from leading hypothesis toward design selection only if the multi-scene test provides evidence that it:

1. preserves or improves literal task clarity;
2. improves contextual orientation/continuity;
3. supports reconsideration without failure coding;
4. supports retrieval and transfer;
5. does not increase unnecessary visual/cognitive load;
6. remains accessible in low-stimulation/reduced-motion form;
7. remains age-respectful;
8. requires no additional personal data or tracking;
9. does not become an adventure/reward economy;
10. scales across competence territories without forcing artificial journey language.

Until then, **Journey/Crossroads remains a leading hypothesis, not the canonical Atlas Percorsi metaphor.**
