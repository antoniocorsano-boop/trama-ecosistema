## Purpose

Cross-review the controlled Literal ↔ Journey/Crossroads storyboard against the governed G1 evidence and safeguards before any richer visual production.

## Review domains

### 1. Pedagogical coherence — PASS

The sequence preserves an explicit relation among situation, missing information, bounded choice, consequence, reconsideration, strategy recognition and transfer. The transfer step deliberately changes surface context so that simple repetition of a social script is insufficient.

**Control retained:** visual atmosphere must never replace the literal learning objective.

### 2. Child safety and emotional development — PASS WITH ACTIVE CONTROL

The storyboard uses a fictional ordinary situation and requires no autobiographical or emotionally intimate disclosure. It does not infer sadness, anxiety, empathy, collaboration or personality from learner behaviour.

**Active control:** character expressions must remain restrained. The learner must never need to interpret a face to solve the task. Feedback concerns action and consequence, never identity or moral worth.

### 3. Privacy and data minimisation — PASS

The pedagogical sequence requires no account, learner name, persistent profile, camera, microphone, location, personal upload or server-side reflection. Choice state can remain ephemeral/local; the final trace is learner-controlled and non-identifying.

**Blocking rule:** adding behavioural analytics, individual engagement telemetry or cloud persistence is outside this approval and requires separate governance.

### 4. Cognitive load / attention — CONDITIONAL PASS

The storyboard explicitly limits novelty and preserves stable anchors. Journey may improve orientation, but persistent route/context elements can themselves become competing stimuli.

**Condition:** in the visual prototype, route/context salience must remain below the current pedagogical target except at the decision/reconsideration frames where spatial structure is the variable under test.

### 5. Visual memory — CONDITIONAL PASS

The design avoids full-scene replacement across the first decision cycle and therefore directly addresses the previously identified image-replacement risk.

**Condition:** validation must distinguish recall of scenery/iconography from recall of the causal/strategic relation. Visual recall alone is not evidence of learning.

### 6. Metaphor dependence — PASS AS TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS

Literal instructions remain authoritative and complete. Journey can theoretically be removed without making the task unintelligible.

**Failure threshold:** if the learner needs road/path decoding to understand the educational action, Journey fails as a scaffold.

### 7. Accessibility — CONDITIONAL PASS

A low-stimulation/reduced-motion equivalent is specified from the outset. Text labels remain primary and colour cannot carry meaning alone.

**Conditions for rendering:** no precision dragging; predictable focus order; adequate touch targets; labels remain associated during zoom/reflow; consequence can be revisited; no audio-only information; no facial-expression dependency.

### 8. Agency — PASS

Progression is learner-controlled; reconsideration is non-punitive; keep/revise are legitimate actions; the final trace can be kept or discarded.

**Control retained:** no streak, score, forced completion, countdown, social comparison or reward economy.

### 9. Sustainability / proportionality — CONDITIONAL PASS

The stable-scene approach supports asset reuse and reduces pressure for scene-by-scene generation. The educational hypothesis does not require video, 3D, ambient audio or continuous animation.

**Condition:** richer media must justify a specific causal/orientation function. Decorative computation/network use is not authorised by this storyboard.

### 10. Age respectfulness — CONDITIONAL PASS

The later-primary framing can remain concrete without requiring fantasy quest language or mascots.

**Condition:** the restrained visual prototype must avoid babyish iconography and moral hero/villain coding. This result cannot be extrapolated automatically to lower secondary.

## Mandatory visual prototype constraints

The next visual artefact may proceed only as a **restrained structural rendering**. It must:

1. render the same exact later-primary scenario in Literal and Journey conditions;
2. show Frames 01–06 using one stable base composition per condition;
3. keep all learner-facing instructional text semantically identical;
4. make the missing-information marker more salient than decorative context;
5. use neutral route geometry and no correct-answer colour coding;
6. avoid expressive emotional faces, mascots, fantasy scenery, rewards and badges;
7. introduce the strategy-tool marker only at Frame 06;
8. include a low-stimulation form in the same design exercise;
9. annotate any visual difference with its pedagogical purpose;
10. remain non-production and non-child-facing.

## What the prototype must NOT attempt yet

- cinematic atmosphere;
- soundscape;
- animation system;
- gamification;
- personalised adaptation;
- learner analytics;
- canonical metaphor selection;
- implementation/runtime.

## Review decision

**CONDITIONAL PASS FOR RESTRAINED VISUAL PROTOTYPE.**

No blocker exists in the storyboard logic itself. The remaining risks are primarily visual and empirical: answer bias, attentional competition, metaphor dependence, age tone and transfer. These cannot be resolved by further prose alone; they require a tightly controlled visual comparison and later appropriately governed human validation.
