# Atlas Percorsi — Child-Safe Learning & Experience Constitution v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery / industrialisation  
**Status:** CANONICAL G1 CONSTITUTION — PROPOSED FOR REVIEW  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED  
**Date:** 2026-09-25

## 1. Purpose

This constitution defines the non-negotiable learning, developmental, attention, emotional-safety, privacy and experience constraints for child-facing Atlas Percorsi.

It does **not** define one universal pathway, visual style, narrative metaphor, character system or interaction grammar. Pathways may differ substantially while remaining inside this constitutional boundary.

## 2. Authority and provenance

This constitution consolidates the current governed G1 findings from:

- `research/common/transfer-of-learning-screening-v1.md`;

A rule appearing here is a **product/design constraint**, not a claim that a particular intervention has been proven effective in Atlas. Effectiveness requires pathway-specific validation.

## 3. Constitutional principles

### C1 — Competence is performance in context, not identity

Atlas Percorsi observes what a learner does in a bounded task. It must not convert task behaviour into a stable psychological, personality or developmental label.

Permitted evidence includes strategy selected, evidence used, revision, changed-context adaptation, explanation and support required.

Prohibited inference includes labels such as impulsive, anxious, empathetic, resilient, mature, gifted, poor collaborator, self-regulated or equivalent identity-like classifications derived from pathway behaviour.

### C2 — The learner retains agency

The experience must preserve meaningful learner choice where the competence calls for choice. The system may scaffold, cue, explain and allow reconsideration; it must not manipulate continuation or manufacture consent through interface pressure.

No dark patterns, artificial urgency, loss-framed streaks, manipulative scarcity, coercive countdowns, infinite engagement loops or emotional dependency mechanisms are permitted.

### C3 — Error is information, not judgement

Incorrect or incomplete performance must remain recoverable where pedagogically coherent. Feedback should identify consequences, missing evidence, constraints or alternative strategies rather than judging the child.

Shame, humiliation, fear and moralised failure are prohibited as motivational mechanisms.

### C4 — Reflection is task-bound and non-invasive

Metacognitive reflection should concern the current task, evidence, strategy, decision or revision. The learner must not be pressured to disclose intimate biography, family circumstances, health, distress, relationships, identity or other sensitive information to make a pathway work.

Fictional, hypothetical, iconic, low-text and non-verbal reflection forms should remain available when pedagogically appropriate.

### C5 — Developmental appropriateness is mandatory

Complexity, abstraction, language, number of simultaneous alternatives, planning horizon, social perspective-taking and reflection format must be deliberately matched to the intended developmental band and then validated with real users.

Earlier-primary, later-primary and lower-secondary distinctions are working design bands, not diagnostic norms. Age alone must not be treated as proof of readiness or deficiency.

### C6 — Avoidable executive burden is removed

The interface must not unnecessarily consume working memory, inhibitory control or navigation effort that should be available for the competence task.

Relevant goals, constraints, evidence and prior-state information should be externally inspectable when remembering them is not itself the intended competence.

Speed is not a default proxy for competence.

### C7 — Attention serves meaning

Atlas Percorsi optimises for meaningful cognitive activity, not maximum stimulation or time-on-platform.

Every salient image, animation, sound, character and interaction requires a functional role: structural, orienting, narrative-causal, affective-safe, accessibility-related, or otherwise explicitly justified.

Decorative novelty has no automatic right to remain.

### C8 — Cognitive continuity is protected

Related steps should preserve stable visual and conceptual anchors where this supports orientation and mental-model construction. Rapid replacement of memorable but weakly integrated imagery is treated as a design risk requiring justification and testing.

Reward effects and animation must not interrupt reasoning or erase information the learner still needs.

### C9 — Interaction must be cognitively consequential

Clicks, taps, dragging, points, completion and time-on-task are not evidence of learning by themselves.

Interaction is justified when it supports selecting, organising, comparing, generating, explaining, deciding, integrating, planning, testing or revising relevant information.

### C10 — Narrative is functional, not compulsory

There is no universal Atlas metaphor, mascot or story world. Narrative may organise causality, perspective, uncertainty, social information, consequence or emotional distance when these functions serve the competence.

Manga/comic, animation, simulation, inquiry, workshop, theatre/viewpoint, map/exploration and other traditions remain pathway-level design resources. None is a default merely because it is attractive to young users.

### C11 — Characters cannot become pseudo-therapists

Characters may externalise viewpoints, uncertainty, frustration, disagreement or competing goals. They must not solicit confidential disclosure, diagnose emotion, simulate exclusive intimacy, claim to understand the learner psychologically or create dependency as a retention mechanism.

### C12 — Transfer must be designed and tested explicitly

Completion of a pathway or repeated success in an unchanged interaction does not establish transversal competence.

Where transfer is claimed, the pathway must specify source context, target context, changed dimensions, invariant structure, support level, observable action and claim ceiling.

Changed-context opportunities should vary selected features deliberately rather than merely changing decoration or changing everything at once.

### C13 — Claims remain calibrated

A successful task permits a statement about that performance. It does not by itself permit a broad claim about the learner.

Farther-transfer claims require stronger evidence. Engagement, compliance, points, speed, repeated accuracy or pathway completion cannot be silently promoted to competence certification.

### C14 — Privacy is architectural

Child-facing Percorsi should work without requiring student accounts, real names or personal biography unless a separately governed future use case proves necessity.

No behavioural profile, cross-pathway psychological dossier or hidden psychometric score may be created from interaction traces.

Any future server-side telemetry proposal requires explicit necessity, minimisation, retention, child-safety, privacy and governance review before implementation.

### C15 — Accessibility is part of correctness

A pathway is not complete if its meaning or essential action depends exclusively on one sensory channel, fine motor precision, avoidable motion, colour alone, inaccessible text or interaction that cannot be reasonably adapted.

Accessibility review must consider comprehension as well as technical operability. Alternative representations must preserve the competence construct rather than accidentally simplifying or changing what is being assessed/practised.

### C16 — Emotional safety does not mean removing challenge

Percorsi may include uncertainty, disagreement, frustration, competing goals and consequential decisions when pedagogically necessary. Challenge must be bounded, recoverable and separated from humiliation, threat or personal exposure.

The learner should be able to exit an emotionally uncomfortable fictional scenario without punitive framing.

### C17 — The teacher remains pedagogically sovereign

Atlas Percorsi does not autonomously diagnose, prescribe remediation, certify psychological traits or replace professional judgement. Pathway evidence is interpretable educational evidence bounded by its task and claim ceiling.

Future teacher-facing views must distinguish observed performance from system inference and make provenance/limitations inspectable.

## 4. Constitutional interpretation of “starting from the self”

The safe Atlas interpretation is:

`I notice → I choose → I inspect evidence/consequence → I reconsider → I explain or adapt my strategy`

The prohibited interpretation is:

`the system learns who I am → infers my personality/emotions → stores a profile → changes future treatment based on hidden psychological classification`

The first supports agency and metacognition. The second crosses the constitutional boundary.

## 5. Functional demand model

Every scene or interaction should be reviewed across four demand classes:

1. **task demand** — inherent complexity of the competence problem;
2. **representation demand** — effort required to decode words, images, symbols, audio or animation;
3. **navigation demand** — effort required to operate the interface;
4. **narrative demand** — effort required to retain actors, events, viewpoints and causal context.

Avoidable representation and navigation demand should be minimised so resources remain available for task-relevant and meaning-relevant processing.

No universal numeric threshold is authorised.

## 6. Pathway constitutional review — required questions

Before a pathway can advance beyond research/prototype status, reviewers must be able to answer:

1. What competence performance is this pathway designed to elicit or support?
2. What must the learner actually think, decide, create, compare or revise?
3. Which demands are inherent to the competence and which are interface-created?
4. What developmental assumptions are being made, and what evidence supports them?
5. Which visual/media elements are functional and which are atmospheric/decorative?
6. Does the experience preserve cognitive continuity?
7. Can an error be recovered from without shame or identity judgement?
8. Does reflection remain task-bound and non-invasive?
9. Could any recorded behaviour be misread as a personality/psychological trait?
10. Does the pathway require personal data that could be removed?
11. Are accessibility alternatives construct-equivalent?
12. If transfer is claimed, exactly what context changed and what remained invariant?
13. What is the strongest claim the observed evidence legitimately permits?
14. What does the teacher need to see to interpret the result responsibly?
15. What would falsify the design hypothesis?

Failure on C1, C2, C3, C4, C11 or C14 is a **constitutional stop**, not a cosmetic defect.

## 7. Evidence and claim states

Pathway propositions should use the governed evidence vocabulary:

- `SUPPORTED` — sufficient relevant evidence for a bounded design direction;
- `UNCERTAIN` — plausible but insufficiently established;
- `UNSUPPORTED` — evidence does not justify the proposed claim;
- `CONTRAINDICATED` — conflicts with evidence, child-safety or constitutional constraints.

Product validation must additionally distinguish:

- `DESIGN_HYPOTHESIS`;
- `PROTOTYPE_OBSERVATION`;
- `ATLAS_VALIDATED_LOCAL_EFFECT`;
- `TRANSFER_EVIDENCE`;
- `NOT_ESTABLISHED`.

These states prevent external research, prototype success and Atlas-specific evidence from being conflated.

## 8. Non-authorised patterns

This constitution does not authorise:

- psychological/personality profiling;
- emotional diagnosis;
- automated developmental classification;
- hidden psychometric scoring;
- universal SEL or executive-function scores;
- learning-styles matching;
- compulsory gamification;
- universal mascots;
- engagement-time optimisation as a learning objective;
- fixed universal media quotas or cognitive-load thresholds;
- competence certification from pathway completion alone;
- personal disclosure as a condition for meaningful reflection;
- broad transfer claims from one changed scenario;
- autonomous remediation based on inferred child traits.

## 9. Relationship with pathway freedom

The constitution deliberately protects creative diversity. A pathway may be quiet or playful, realistic or fictional, text-light or dialogue-rich, comic-like or simulation-based, individual or collaborative.

Freedom exists **inside** the constitutional constraints. Therefore a pathway-specific narrative identity is compatible with a shared Atlas safety and learning architecture.

## 10. Governance and change control

- This document is canonical only at G1 and remains subject to human review.
- It does not authorise runtime implementation.
- A pathway cannot weaken a constitutional rule locally.
- A proposed exception must identify the exact rule, evidence, child-safety implications and governance authority required.
- New evidence may strengthen, refine or retire a rule through an explicit governed revision; silent drift is prohibited.
- Research provenance must remain traceable to evidence packets/records as normalisation proceeds.

## 11. Promotion criteria

Promotion from `PROPOSED FOR REVIEW` to an approved constitutional baseline requires at minimum:

1. reconciliation with existing privacy-first TRAMA/Atlas governance;
2. accessibility review, including reduced-motion and alternative-representation implications;
3. child-safety/privacy review;
4. pedagogical review across primary and lower-secondary scope;
5. provenance mapping to normalised evidence records;
6. review of compatibility with pathway-specific narrative architecture;
7. human exact-head review.

## 12. Immediate implementation consequence

Until promotion is complete, new Atlas Percorsi prototypes should voluntarily conform to this constitution, but conformity does not authorise production runtime.

The next engineering/research work is to create a machine-checkable **Pathway Constitution Review Contract** for dossier validation while normalising the evidence register. Automated checks may verify declared fields and prohibited configurations; they must not pretend to replace pedagogical or child-safety judgement.
