# Atlas Percorsi — Storytelling-First Authoring Contract v1

**Status:** PROPOSED_CANONICAL_AUTHORING_CONTRACT / HUMAN_DIRECTION_APPROVED  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED  
**Applies to:** learner-facing Atlas Percorsi  
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## 1. Decision

For first-cycle learner-facing Percorsi, **storytelling is an authoring artefact that precedes interface design**.

The project must not begin from:
- cards;
- screens;
- UI components;
- quizzes;
- interaction widgets;
- generic “missions” added after the pedagogical structure is fixed.

The default authoring sequence becomes:

`COMPETENCE TARGET → STORYTELLING CONCEPT → STORY HUMAN REVIEW → WORLD BRIEF → ROLE/AGENCY MAP → INFORMATION DISTRIBUTION → CONSEQUENCE MODEL → EXPERIENCE CHOREOGRAPHY → VISUAL WORLD CONCEPT → PLAYABLE PROTOTYPE → HUMAN USE REVIEW → IMPLEMENTATION`

## 2. Why storytelling comes first

The storytelling artefact provides the structure that answers:

- who exists in the experience;
- what they want;
- what is already happening before the learner arrives;
- what changes or goes wrong;
- why the learner has a reason to participate;
- how information is revealed;
- what tension/curiosity carries the learner forward;
- what consequences make later actions meaningful.

Without these elements, interaction tends to collapse into a school exercise with decorative context.

## 3. Storytelling is not decoration

A valid storytelling concept is not:
- a title;
- a paragraph of premise;
- an avatar skin;
- a theme pasted over an exercise;
- a sequence of didactic instructions spoken by characters.

A valid storytelling concept must survive as a **coherent story** even before UI and mechanics are designed.

## 4. Minimum storytelling anatomy

Every storytelling candidate must define:

1. **Hook / opening image**
2. **World / setting**
3. **Characters**
4. **Character goals**
5. **Initial normal state**
6. **Disruption / problem**
7. **Learner role**
8. **What is unknown**
9. **How information emerges**
10. **Escalation / complication**
11. **Turning point**
12. **Resolution or open ending**
13. **Why the competence becomes useful inside the story**
14. **Emotional register**
15. **Age-appropriateness / dignity boundary**

## 5. Story-first quality gate

Before World Brief or interface work, Human Review must be able to answer YES:

- Does this sound like a story rather than a lesson plan?
- Is there something worth finding out?
- Do characters want or need something independently of the curriculum?
- Does the learner have a plausible reason to participate?
- Could the story be told aloud in a few minutes and still make sense?
- Is there a turning point rather than only a sequence of tasks?
- Can the competence emerge because the story creates a need for it?
- Is there a plausible reason an 11–14-year-old might want to know what happens next?

If not, the concept remains in story development.

## 6. Story collection

All serious storytelling candidates are stored in a shared registry.

Possible states:
- `SEED`
- `STORY_DRAFT`
- `HUMAN_STORY_REVIEW_REQUIRED`
- `STORY_APPROVED_FOR_WORLD_DESIGN`
- `WORLD_DESIGN_ACTIVE`
- `SUPERSEDED`
- `REJECTED`

A story may be preserved as a seed even if it is not selected for a current Percorso.

## 7. Reuse

Story worlds may be reused across multiple Percorsi only if:
- the world remains coherent;
- the new competence produces a genuinely different problem/action;
- reuse does not make characters artificial teaching devices;
- continuity benefits the learner.

Reuse is optional, not a requirement.

## 8. Non-narrative exception

A first-cycle Percorso may omit explicit narrative only when Human Review records why a non-narrative experiential form is stronger for the target.

The exception must still provide:
- meaningful context;
- role or functional purpose;
- agency;
- consequence;
- desire-to-continue rationale.

## 9. Separation from screenplay

Storytelling answers:
> What happens, to whom, and why should I care?

Experience choreography answers:
> How does the learner encounter and affect that story?

Screenplay/UI answers:
> How is the experience staged moment by moment?

Do not collapse these artefacts.

## 10. Authority boundary

Story approval:
- does not authorize implementation;
- does not authorize runtime;
- does not approve territory taxonomy;
- does not replace child-safety/accessibility/privacy review;
- does not imply Q9.

It authorizes progression to world design only.
