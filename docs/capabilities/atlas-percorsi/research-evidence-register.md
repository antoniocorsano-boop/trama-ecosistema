# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — Research & Evidence Register

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery  
**Status:** IN_PROGRESS / AUTHORITATIVE_BASELINE_EXPANDED  
**Rule:** research is evidence, not approval.

## Evidence quality rules

1. Prefer primary legislation, official institutional frameworks and peer-reviewed synthesis/reviews.
2. Never generalise evidence from adults/university students directly to children without an age/population limitation note.
3. A framework name is not a design decision: record the specific implication it supports.
4. Legal/supervisory material is evidence for G2 analysis, not an automatic legal conclusion.
5. Narrative evidence supports experimentation; it does not justify decorative immersion or engagement maximisation.
6. G1 PASS requires stable sources, population/context and explicit limitations, not bibliography volume.
7. For visual/multimedia evidence distinguish attention capture from learning support: an image can be memorable or attractive without improving comprehension, retention of the intended concept or transfer.

### PED-002 — 2026 systematic review on development of SRL
Systematic review, Learning and Instruction, DOI 10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102433.
Reference: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102433
**Population/context:** research across developmental phases from early childhood through adolescence.
**Finding:** executive functions and metacognitive knowledge become more sophisticated through childhood/primary years, but reported strategy use can decline around transition to secondary school; development is not a simple linear increase with age.
**Atlas applicability:** supports age-band scaffolding and particular attention to the primary→lower-secondary transition. More autonomy should not mean removal of scaffolding.
**Limitation:** constructs and assessment methods vary across studies; findings do not define individual developmental norms or justify profiling.
**State:** VERIFIED_RECENT_SCHOLARLY_SYNTHESIS, 2026-09-25.

### PED-VIS-001 — Cognitive load in multimedia learning: systematic review
Mutlu-Bayraktar, Cosgun & Altan, Computers & Education 141 (2019), 103618; DOI 10.1016/j.compedu.2019.103618.
Reference: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S036013151930171X
**Population/context:** systematic review of 94 multimedia/cognitive-load studies; higher education and STEM are over-represented.
**Finding:** presentation format, modality, seductive details and signaling are recurrent determinants of extraneous cognitive load.
**Atlas applicability:** visual richness must be treated as a cognitive-load variable, not automatically as a benefit. Each visual/motion layer needs an instructional/narrative function and a low-distraction alternative.
**Limitation:** evidence base is not child-dominant; child-specific records are required before age-band rules are frozen.
**State:** VERIFIED_SCHOLARLY_SYNTHESIS, 2026-09-25.

### PED-VIS-002 — Seductive details meta-analysis
Rey, Educational Research Review 7(3) (2012), 216–237; DOI 10.1016/j.edurev.2012.05.003.
Reference: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X12000413
**Finding:** interesting but instructionally irrelevant details can impair retention/transfer; proposed mechanisms include working-memory overload, attentional distraction and coherence disruption.
**Atlas applicability:** attractive images, animation, lore, badges or ambient details must not compete with the learning target. Memorability of an image is not evidence that the intended concept was learned.
**Limitation:** effects vary with design/context; this does not imply a ban on illustration or narrative detail.
**State:** VERIFIED_META_ANALYSIS, 2026-09-25.

### PED-VIS-003 — Signaling text-picture relations meta-analysis
Richter, Scheiter & Eitel, Educational Research Review 17 (2016), 19–36; DOI 10.1016/j.edurev.2015.12.003.
Reference: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X15000664
**Population/context:** 27 studies, 45 comparisons, N=2,464.
**Finding:** signaling relevant text-picture correspondences has a small-to-medium positive effect on comprehension/transfer and particularly supports learners with low prior knowledge.
**Atlas applicability:** use visual hierarchy, cueing and correspondence to direct attention toward meaning; do not force visual search across decorative competing elements.
**Limitation:** not exclusively child samples and signaling quality/context matter.
**State:** VERIFIED_META_ANALYSIS, 2026-09-25.

### PED-VIS-004 — Visual cues in interactive storybooks for children
Bali et al., Journal of Experimental Child Psychology 260 (2025), 106320; DOI 10.1016/j.jecp.2025.106320.
Reference: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022096525001262
**Population/context:** 119 children, mean age 9.35 years, science-themed interactive storybook/video/static conditions.
**Finding:** interactive/multimedia elements can support learning but can also distract; explicit visual signaling reduced cognitive-load problems, with particular relevance for children with attentional difficulties.
**Atlas applicability:** direct child evidence for guided attention, restrained interactivity and inclusive cueing. Interaction must make the pedagogically relevant object obvious before inviting exploration.
**Limitation:** one age range, one content/task context; not a universal interface prescription.
**State:** VERIFIED_CHILD_SPECIFIC_SCHOLARLY_SOURCE, 2026-09-25.

### PED-VIS-005 — Development of executive function through childhood/adolescence
Best & Miller, Child Development 81(6) (2010), 1641–1660; DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01499.x.
Reference: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3058827/
**Finding:** inhibition, working memory and shifting continue developing substantially through childhood and adolescence, with different trajectories.
**Atlas applicability:** do not design as if attention control, working memory, inhibition and task switching were adult-stable capacities. Reduce simultaneous demands and preserve reorientation/recovery.
**Limitation:** developmental review, not a direct test of Atlas or a diagnostic basis for individuals.
**State:** VERIFIED_SCHOLARLY_REVIEW, 2026-09-25.

## Negative knowledge

- **NK-001 Numerical identity scoring:** candidate rejection.
- **NK-003 Data-bearing personalisation:** candidate rejection; personalisation ≠ server-side identity/tracking.
- **NK-004 Library-first design:** candidate rejection.
- **NK-005 Age-uniform experience:** candidate rejection; same interaction across entire first cycle is not presumed developmentally appropriate.
- **NK-006 Immersion-as-value:** candidate rejection; more animation/sound/narrative density ≠ better learning.
- **NK-007 Metaphor-as-required-decoder:** candidate rejection; learner must understand task and safety choices without interpreting metaphor.
- **NK-008 Atlas-as-curriculum-authority:** candidate rejection; national/institute curriculum authority remains outside the public experience layer and must be bound through ecosystem governance.
- **NK-009 Image-replacement treadmill:** NEW candidate rejection. Do not design rapid sequences of attractive images that primarily capture attention and overwrite one another without requiring integration, retrieval, reflection or transfer. Visual novelty is not a learning objective.
- **NK-010 Decorative interactivity:** NEW candidate rejection. Do not add taps, motion, hotspots, animation or rewards merely to maintain engagement; interaction must expose meaning, support choice/action, or provide pedagogically relevant feedback.

## Visual attention / memory design hypothesis for G4 validation

The user's field observation that children may strongly attend to images and then replace one salient image with the next is recorded as a **design hypothesis**, not as a universal cognitive fact. Evidence supports the underlying risks of limited working memory, developing executive control, distraction by irrelevant visual details and benefits of signaling. Therefore candidate safeguards are:

1. **One pedagogical focal point per scene/state** unless comparison/integration explicitly requires more.
2. **Stable visual anchors:** key symbols/objects persist long enough to acquire meaning rather than being replaced for novelty.
3. **Image → action/reflection bridge:** important imagery should lead to a choice, explanation, retrieval, manipulation or transfer task rather than passive viewing alone.
4. **Progressive disclosure:** reveal complexity only when needed; avoid simultaneous competing stimuli.
5. **Signaling over decoration:** visual contrast/motion points to relevant information, not merely to what is visually exciting.
6. **User-paced transitions by default:** avoid automatic scene churn/autoplay for learning-critical content.
7. **Pause and reorientation:** allow the learner to stop, revisit the current objective and recover context without penalty.
8. **Reduced-motion/low-stimulation equivalent:** preserve meaning and task completion without animation/sound.
9. **No memory inference from attraction:** clicking, dwelling on or recalling an image must not be interpreted as competence or understanding.
10. **Age-band and inclusion validation:** test with intended learners, including attention/learning differences, without profiling individuals.

These are G1/G4 candidate constraints. They require prototype validation and must not be converted into clinical claims about children.

## G1 completion rule

G1 does not pass because sources have been collected. It passes only when evidence supports a reviewable pedagogical/domain model, age progression, child-safety/privacy constraints, accessibility/inclusion constraints, narrative decision criteria, documented alternatives/negative knowledge and explicit open questions. Every material conclusion remains traceable to source, population/context, limitation and governed implication.
