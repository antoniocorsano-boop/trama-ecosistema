# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — Pathway-Specific Narrative Architecture v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery  
**Status:** CONSOLIDATED_ARCHITECTURAL_HYPOTHESIS / NOT_RUNTIME_AUTHORIZATION  
**Scope:** Atlas Percorsi trasversali  
**Supersedes as design hypothesis:** search for one universal Atlas Percorsi metaphor

## Decision to preserve

Atlas Percorsi should **not** force every transversal competence pathway into one universal metaphor, story world or character system.

The governed direction is instead:

> **One common educational/safety grammar; pathway-specific narrative identities.**

Atlas provides the constitutional rules and narrative grammar. Each pathway derives its own subject, metaphorical devices, world, characters, atmosphere and rhythm from the competence being developed, the age band and the pedagogical situation.

This decision responds to the controlled experiments: the strongest value observed in Journey/Crossroads was not literal travel but **contextual continuity**. That continuity can be achieved through different narrative architectures.

## Four-layer model

### Layer 1 — Atlas Percorsi Constitution

Non-negotiable across every pathway:

- child safety and age-appropriate design;
- no psychological diagnosis or emotional profiling;
- no required sensitive autobiographical disclosure;
- no student account/profile/tracking requirement;
- local/ephemeral state by default;
- accessibility and low-stimulation equivalence;
- learner agency: pause, skip where pedagogically legitimate, revise, recover orientation;
- feedback on actions/strategies, never identity or personal worth;
- attention proportionality: no stimulation merely to retain engagement;
- consequence-aware sustainability and asset proportionality;
- teacher/adult mediation classification where needed;
- literal comprehension must not depend on metaphor.

### Layer 2 — Common Narrative Grammar

Reusable functions, not mandatory visual forms:

`CONTEXT → ORIENT → NOTICE → ACT/CHOOSE → CONSEQUENCE → RECONSIDER → NAME/RECOGNISE STRATEGY → CHANGED CONTEXT → TRANSFER → LEARNER-CONTROLLED TRACE`

A pathway may omit a function when pedagogically unnecessary, but must document why.

Common local narrative devices may include:

- **crossroads** when a genuine consequential decision exists;
- **tool** when a reusable strategy has been recognised;
- **lens/viewpoint** when perspective-taking is the cognitive action;
- **map** when orientation among relations/options is necessary;
- **trace/clue** when inquiry and evidence are central;
- **shared construction** when coordination/contribution is central.

No device becomes a universal Atlas ornament.

### Layer 3 — Pathway Identity

Each pathway must define its own:

1. transversal competence target;
2. pedagogical rationale;
3. age-band adaptation;
4. subject/premise;
5. world/context;
6. narrative engine — what makes one situation lead meaningfully to the next;
7. character system and point-of-view roles;
8. local metaphorical devices;
9. atmosphere and visual rhythm;
10. information-release rhythm;
11. emotional-intensity boundary;
12. mediation level;
13. transfer design;
14. accessibility/low-stimulation equivalent;
15. privacy/data state;
16. sustainability/resource budget;
17. failure conditions.

### Layer 4 — Individual Experience

The individual experience contains the concrete screenplay/storyboard:

- scenes/frames;
- literal learner instructions;
- interactions;
- consequences;
- reconsideration points;
- strategy recognition;
- transfer tasks;
- local/exportable artefacts where governed;
- accessibility states;
- exact data handling.

## Pathway differentiation principle

The narrative identity must follow the **structure of the competence**, not a desire for visual variety.

Illustrative hypotheses, not approved pathway specifications:

| Competence territory | Potential narrative logic | Candidate local devices |
|---|---|---|
| self-awareness / metacognition | observe, compare, reframe, recognise strategies | viewpoint, lens, notebook/trace |
| decision and responsibility | alternatives, trade-offs, consequences, revision | crossroads, map, consequence markers |
| collaboration | partial perspectives/contributions becoming coordinated action | shared construction, roles, tools |
| critical thinking / information literacy | clues, missing information, source comparison, hypothesis revision | traces, evidence board, lens, map |
| communication / perspective-taking | same event seen/heard from different positions | character viewpoints, framing, sequence |
| problem solving | understand a system, intervene, inspect effects, adapt | workshop, system map, tools |

These mappings must be tested; they are not templates to apply automatically.

## Character architecture

Characters are **cognitive/narrative actors**, not mascots or personality labels.

Each significant character requires a small governed character bible describing:

- narrative function;
- what the character knows and does not know;
- viewpoint and information access;
- relation to the learner's task;
- language/register appropriate to age;
- allowed development across scenes;
- emotional-expression limits;
- stereotypes/identity shortcuts to avoid;
- whether the character persists across experiences or belongs only to one pathway.

Avoid fixed reductive types such as “the shy one”, “the clever one”, “the difficult one”, “the anxious one”. A temporary behaviour in a scene must not become identity.

## Japanese visual-narrative research: architectural consequence

The Japanese research strand is not being used to justify a generic “manga style”. Its value is to investigate reusable narrative principles such as:

- sequential construction of meaning across images;
- control of information release;
- panel/scene rhythm and ellipsis;
- continuity with selective change;
- characters as carriers of viewpoint/information;
- visual framing as attention guidance;
- active interpretation by the reader;
- age-sensitive visual literacy.

These principles should be evaluated **per pathway**. A perspective-taking pathway may benefit more from character viewpoint/framing; an inquiry pathway may benefit more from information sequencing and ellipsis; a decision pathway may benefit more from spatial alternatives and consequences.

Population/context caution remains mandatory: findings from Japanese educational/cultural contexts are evidence to interpret, not direct prescriptions for Italian primary/lower-secondary learners.

## Research programme update

Future literature work should therefore have two levels:

### Common research

Research that can govern all pathways:

- child development and visual narrative comprehension;
- cognitive load and multimedia learning;
- attention and memory;
- emotional development/safety;
- privacy and child-rights-by-design;
- accessibility;
- visual literacy;
- narrative comprehension and transfer.

### Pathway-specific research

For every new pathway, add a research packet addressing:

- competence-specific pedagogy;
- age-specific difficulties/misconceptions;
- suitable narrative structures;
- character/perspective needs;
- emotional risks;
- transfer evidence;
- relevant cultural/narrative precedents, including Japanese literature where useful;
- risks of metaphor mismatch.

Research findings, reasoning, uncertainty and design consequences remain separate records when materially important.

## Required pathway dossier

A new Atlas Percorsi pathway cannot proceed directly from idea to interface. It should create/update a dossier containing at minimum:

1. `pathway-brief` — competence, population, need, boundaries;
2. `evidence-register` — pedagogical/normative/safety/accessibility evidence;
3. `research-reasoning` — significant interpretation connecting evidence to design;
4. `narrative-concept` — subject, world, narrative engine, metaphor rationale;
5. `character-bible` — only where characters are material;
6. `safeguard-profile` — privacy, emotional safety, mediation, accessibility;
7. `storyboard` — controlled scenes and interactions;
8. `consequence-review` — foreseeable benefits/harms and controls;
9. `validation-plan` — what evidence would support/reject the design;
10. `decision-log` — accepted, rejected and deferred hypotheses.

This is the repeatable model intended to prevent later reconstruction and documentation debt.

## Design-system consequence

Atlas Percorsi needs a **shared structural design system with variable narrative skins**, not one identical AI-generated interface.

Shared components may include:

- orientation/progress semantics;
- choice and reconsideration controls;
- consequence presentation;
- strategy trace;
- accessibility controls;
- local-save/export affordances;
- mediation indicators;
- typography/readability/touch rules.

Pathway-variable elements may include:

- scene composition;
- world/setting;
- character cast;
- metaphorical devices;
- illustration grammar;
- rhythm and transitions;
- atmosphere.

Variation must not change safety semantics or interaction predictability.

## Governance rule for a new idea

When a new pathway idea appears:

`IDEA → CLASSIFY COMPETENCE → CHECK EXISTING EVIDENCE → OPEN/UPDATE PATHWAY DOSSIER → RESEARCH GAPS → RECORD REASONING → DEFINE NARRATIVE HYPOTHESES → SAFEGUARD REVIEW → CONTROLLED STORYBOARD → VALIDATION → ONLY THEN PRODUCT SPEC/IMPLEMENTATION`

No polished visual should become the de facto specification before this chain is sufficiently complete.

## Current status

- **Universal Journey metaphor:** no longer the target hypothesis.
- **Contextual/narrative continuity:** retained as a strong cross-pathway principle to test.
- **Journey/Crossroads:** local or pathway-level device where decision/consequence structure warrants it.
- **Workshop/Tools:** local device for reusable strategies where appropriate.
- **Japanese research:** retained and expanded as a source of narrative/character/sequential-design principles, applied selectively per pathway.
- **Canonical Atlas Percorsi identity:** shared constitution + grammar + interaction semantics; narrative identity differentiated by pathway.
