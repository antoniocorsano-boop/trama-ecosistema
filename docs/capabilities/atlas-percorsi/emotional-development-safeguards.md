# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — Emotional Development & Learning Safeguards

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery  
**Status:** EVIDENCE_BOUND / DESIGN_CONSTRAINTS_CANDIDATE  
**Audience:** minors across primary and lower-secondary education  
**Runtime authorization:** NONE

## Purpose

Atlas Percorsi may address self-awareness, relationships, choices, frustration, uncertainty, collaboration and other experiences with an emotional dimension. Because the users are minors, emotions cannot be treated as decorative engagement variables, as data to extract, or as psychological traits to classify.

The design objective is educational: help learners recognise, name, regulate, contextualise and reflect on emotions when pedagogically relevant, while preserving dignity, privacy, agency and appropriate adult mediation.

## Evidence boundary

Current institutional evidence supports treating social-emotional development as connected with learning and well-being, and supports deliberate educational opportunities to develop relevant skills. It does **not** justify diagnosing a child, inferring mental state from interaction traces, assigning stable emotional/personality labels, or assuming that a digital experience is therapeutic.

OECD evidence also shows meaningful variation by age, context and socio-demographic conditions. Group-level findings must never be converted into expectations or predictions about an individual learner.

UNICEF's RITEC work reinforces a child-centred digital-design view in which well-being includes safety/security, inclusion, autonomy, emotions, competence, relationships, creativity and identity. These dimensions interact: maximising engagement is not the governing objective.

## Governing distinction

Atlas must distinguish:

`EDUCATIONAL EMOTIONAL LITERACY ≠ PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT ≠ THERAPY ≠ EMOTION PROFILING`

A learning activity may invite a learner to recognise or reflect on an emotion. It must not claim to determine what the learner "really feels", why they feel it, or what kind of person they are.

## Emotional safety baseline

### 1. No compelled disclosure

A minor must not be required to disclose private emotional experiences, family circumstances, health information, traumatic experiences, conflicts, identity information or other sensitive personal material in order to progress.

Where reflection is useful, provide safe alternatives such as:

- fictional or third-person situations;
- choosing among non-diagnostic examples;
- private/local reflection not transmitted to Atlas;
- the option to skip a personal prompt without penalty;
- discussing with a trusted adult/teacher when the activity is designed for mediated use.

### 2. Fiction before confession

When a competence can be exercised through a fictional scenario, prefer the fictional scenario over asking the learner to expose a personal experience. Transfer to the learner's life may be invited later and privately, not demanded.

### 3. Emotion is information, not identity

Use formulations equivalent to "in this situation a person might feel..." rather than "you are anxious/angry/insecure". Emotional states are contextual and changing; they must not become badges, profiles, scores or persistent labels.

### 4. No hidden inference

Do not infer emotion, vulnerability, personality or psychological condition from:

- response time;
- click/touch behaviour;
- navigation patterns;
- writing style;
- camera/microphone signals;
- device sensors;
- choice history;
- generated artefacts.

Any future proposal in this area would require a new, explicit governance cycle and cannot inherit authorization from Atlas Percorsi.

### 5. No emotional manipulation

Narrative, imagery, sound, animation, scarcity, streaks, rewards or character attachment must not be designed to induce guilt, fear, shame, dependency, urgency or disclosure in order to increase engagement or completion.

### 6. Dignified error and retry

Wrong choices in a learning scenario must not humiliate or morally label the learner. Feedback should describe the consequence, offer another perspective or enable retry. Avoid "good/bad child" framing.

### 7. Regulation before acceleration

If a scene intentionally introduces uncertainty, conflict or frustration, it must also provide sufficient orientation, pacing and recovery. The interface must not immediately replace an emotionally salient image or situation with another stimulus merely to maintain attention.

## Cognitive-emotional coupling

Emotion, attention, memory and learning interact. Therefore emotional intensity is also a cognitive-load variable.

Candidate design rule:

`EMOTIONAL CUE → ORIENTATION → MEANINGFUL CHOICE/ACTION → PAUSE → REFLECTION/RECALL → TRANSFER`

not:

`EMOTIONAL CUE → REWARD/STIMULUS → NEW CUE → NEW STIMULUS`.

Each emotionally salient scene should document:

- why emotion is pedagogically relevant;
- expected intensity range;
- what the learner is asked to notice or do;
- what remains private;
- how the learner can pause/skip/reorient;
- what recovery or debrief follows;
- whether adult mediation is advisable;
- how the same objective can be reached with lower emotional intensity.

## Age-sensitive direction

### Earlier primary

Prefer:

- concrete, familiar and non-threatening situations;
- simple emotional vocabulary with contextual cues;
- short cycles;
- visible options rather than open autobiographical writing;
- fictional characters/situations as protective distance;
- strong orientation and predictable interaction;
- optional adult/teacher scaffolding.

Avoid assuming that an attractive image demonstrates comprehension.

### Later primary

May progressively introduce:

- comparison between possible perspectives;
- links among event, emotion, choice and consequence;
- multiple plausible responses;
- simple regulation strategies in context;
- reflection on what changed after trying a strategy.

Personal disclosure remains optional/private.

### Lower-secondary

May introduce greater nuance and ambiguity while preserving safeguards:

- conflicting perspectives and trade-offs;
- emotion as one source of information among others;
- autonomy in choosing strategies;
- reflection on social consequences;
- more mature narrative tone without removing scaffolding.

Do not mistake older age for emotional invulnerability or automatic self-regulation.

## Diversity, inclusion and non-normativity

Children differ in language, culture, neurodevelopment, sensory processing, prior experience, disability, family context and emotional expression. Atlas must not prescribe one "correct" facial expression, reaction speed, social style or emotional response as proof of competence.

The same pedagogical objective should permit multiple legitimate ways to engage where possible.

## Adult mediation boundary

Atlas is not a substitute for a teacher, family relationship, counsellor, clinician or safeguarding process.

A pathway must declare one of:

- `INDEPENDENT_PUBLIC_USE` — safe without adult mediation;
- `OPTIONAL_MEDIATION` — usable independently but enriched by teacher/adult discussion;
- `MEDIATED_ONLY` — activity should only be published within a clearly governed adult-mediated context.

The classification is about the activity, not about profiling the child.

## Safety review questions for every scene

Before a scene can progress to specification/design, answer:

1. Does it ask the learner to reveal something private or sensitive?
2. Could the same objective be achieved through a fictional/third-person scenario?
3. Could imagery, sound, wording or pacing create avoidable fear, shame, pressure or overload?
4. Can the learner skip, pause or recover without penalty?
5. Does feedback describe learning rather than judge identity?
6. Is any emotional information stored, transmitted or inferred?
7. Is adult mediation needed?
8. Are there accessible/lower-stimulation equivalents?
9. Could a learner reasonably misread the activity as diagnosis or counselling?
10. What foreseeable adverse consequence exists, and can it be eliminated rather than merely mitigated?

## Evidence and measurement boundary

Do not measure emotional development merely because digital interaction makes measurement possible. If evidence of learning is required, prefer observable educational actions in the scenario (for example identifying perspectives, selecting/justifying a strategy, revising a choice, transferring a strategy) rather than intimate self-report or emotion scoring.

Any aggregate evaluation of pathway quality must be designed so that it does not create individual student tracking.

## Negative knowledge

### NK-EMO-001 — Confession as pedagogy
Do not require personal emotional disclosure to prove learning.

### NK-EMO-002 — Emotion detection
Do not infer a minor's emotional or psychological state from behavioural or sensor data.

### NK-EMO-003 — Emotional identity labels
Do not convert transient/contextual emotions into stable learner labels or scores.

### NK-EMO-004 — Engagement through emotional pressure
Do not use guilt, shame, fear, urgency, dependency or social comparison as engagement mechanics.

### NK-EMO-005 — Attractive image equals emotional understanding
Do not infer comprehension because a learner attends to, remembers or selects an emotionally salient image.

### NK-EMO-006 — Digital substitute for human care
Do not position Atlas as a replacement for educational relationships, safeguarding or professional support.

## G1 consequence

The forthcoming comparative prototypes for `Journey/Crossroads` and `Laboratory/Tools` must include at least one emotionally meaningful but non-sensitive scenario. Both metaphorical variants and the literal control version must be evaluated against the same safeguards so that atmosphere and narrative can be compared without changing the underlying pedagogical or safety requirement.
