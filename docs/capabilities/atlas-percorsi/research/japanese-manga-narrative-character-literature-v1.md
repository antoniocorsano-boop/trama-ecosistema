**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED

## Research question

What can Japanese research on manga, sequential images, character construction and educational manga contribute to Atlas Percorsi, particularly to the emerging hypothesis of a coherent continuous world with local cognitive metaphors rather than one global Journey metaphor?

## Initial evidence set

### E-JP-01 — Tsuji, 2023, empirical research on children's manga expression

Study of 51 Japanese children aged 6–13 in an extracurricular art setting. With worksheet structure and instructor support, children could understand and enjoy manga panel composition; combining and arranging multiple pictures enabled creation of new meanings/interpretations.

**Relevance:** sequence and relation among images can themselves carry meaning. For Atlas, this supports treating continuity between scenes as a pedagogical grammar rather than producing isolated attractive illustrations.

**Limit:** small contextual sample; this does not establish superiority of manga presentation for Atlas or for all children.

### E-JP-02 — Suzuki, Mochizuki & Kubota, 2010, manga-based thinking

Manga representation was used in teacher education to make lesson activity and learners' voices more vivid and to support revision of lesson plans from learners' viewpoints.

**Relevance:** actors + speech + sequential situations can help designers reason from participants' perspectives. This may be useful not only for the child-facing interface but as an internal storyboard/design-review method.

**Limit:** participants were student teachers, not children using a learning environment.

### E-JP-03 — Daikoku, Takenaka & Inagaki, 2009, manga educational materials

Educational manga for cooperative-learning teacher education was reported as easy to read/review and useful for communicating cooperative-learning methods. The authors highlight manga's ability to control information and permit multiple interpretations.

**Relevance:** information pacing/control is especially important for Atlas because visual novelty must not overwhelm reflection. Sequential framing can reveal information deliberately rather than replacing one full illustration with another.

**Limit:** teacher-education context; results cannot be transferred directly to minors.

### E-JP-04 — Okubo et al., 2016, manga and media literacy

A manga-based media-literacy learning programme for fifth-grade pupils involved reading, interpreting and appreciating manga expression techniques and reported significant pre/post improvements on the programme's media-literacy measures, with higher means than a non-participating comparison class.

**Relevance:** children can be active interpreters of visual/sequential language rather than passive recipients. Atlas should consider making visual grammar inspectable and meaningful, not merely decorative.

**Limit:** the learning objective was media literacy itself; it does not show that manga form improves unrelated transversal competencies.

### E-JP-05 — Kishi, 2020, interest/motivation with manga teaching materials

Research with second-grade pupils found that manga materials could stimulate interest/motivation and identified multiple manga-specific factors contributing to that response.

**Relevance:** manga-like presentation can increase attraction, but for Atlas this is a double-edged result: interest is not equivalent to learning. Attention capture must remain subordinate to comprehension, reflection and transfer.

### E-JP-06 — Takabayashi, 2020, character modelling in learning manga

Art-education research analyses character-forming techniques and their strong presence in learning manga, proposing educational applications including manga literacy.

**Relevance:** character design is not neutral. Shape, expression and visual coding communicate expectations before dialogue is read. Atlas therefore needs a governed character-language specification rather than generic AI-generated children.

**Risk:** character appearance must never be used to imply personality, moral worth, competence or psychological state through stereotypes.

## Initial synthesis for Atlas

### 1. Replace “new image” thinking with sequential meaning

The strongest immediate contribution is not “make Atlas look like manga.” It is to treat **relations between frames** as meaningful. A scene can preserve anchors and alter one element so that the learner sees causality and consequence.

Candidate grammar:

`STABLE CONTEXT → SELECTIVE CHANGE → INTERPRETATION → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE → RETURN/REVISION → RECONTEXTUALISATION`

### 2. Characters should be actors, not mascots

Characters can make viewpoints and consequences legible. They should not exist primarily to entertain, reward, praise or create attachment.

A character should have a pedagogical role such as:

- introduce a viewpoint;
- hold information;
- act on a decision;
- make a consequence observable;
- create legitimate ambiguity;
- support perspective-taking without demanding autobiography.

### 3. Character continuity may reduce cognitive replacement

Rather than replacing the entire image at every step, stable characters and spatial anchors can support continuity. Change should be local and meaningful.

This is a hypothesis to test, not an established effect for Atlas.

### 4. Speech and viewpoint can externalise thinking

Manga's combination of actors and words suggests a way to present multiple viewpoints without asking the learner to reveal personal feelings. Fictional characters can articulate competing reasons, uncertainty or missing information.

This supports the existing child-safety principle of **fictional distance before personal disclosure**.

### 5. Panel/frame boundaries can regulate information

Instead of continuous animation or dense screens, Atlas can use controlled reveal and learner-paced progression. A frame boundary can create a cognitive pause.

This aligns with the current attention safeguard: meaningful stimulus → orientation → action → pause → reflection/recall → transfer.

### 6. Attraction is not the success metric

Japanese research showing interest/motivation effects is useful precisely because Atlas must not confuse attraction with competence development. Manga-derived devices should survive only if they improve comprehension, causal reasoning, perspective-taking, retrieval or transfer.

### 7. “Continuous world + local grammar” gains plausibility

The emerging Atlas hypothesis is strengthened conceptually:

- **continuous coherent world** = narrative/context architecture;
- **characters** = carriers of viewpoints/actions, not mascots;
- **panels/scenes** = pacing and selective information control;
- **crossroads** = local representation when a consequential choice exists;
- **tool/card** = local representation when a reusable strategy is recognised;
- **lens/viewpoint** = local representation when perspective changes;
- **map** = local representation when orientation across experiences is needed.

No single metaphor must carry every competence.

## Character-governance questions derived from the literature

Before defining recurring Atlas characters, investigate:

1. How much visual simplification supports recognition without stereotyping?
2. Which facial/body cues are necessary for narrative comprehension and which become emotional overcoding?
3. How can multiple viewpoints be represented without assigning fixed “types” to characters?
4. Should recurring characters have stable roles, or would stable roles create identity stereotypes?
5. How can character continuity support memory while preventing parasocial/reward mechanics?
6. How should visual language mature from early primary to lower secondary?
7. Can the same character remain recognisable in low-stimulation, monochrome and accessibility variants?
8. How can dialogue balloons/captions remain readable under reflow, zoom and small-screen use?

## Negative knowledge

- Do not imitate manga aesthetics merely because Japanese manga is culturally successful.
- Do not infer educational effectiveness from popularity or visual appeal.
- Do not use exaggerated facial expressions when the learner would then solve the task by reading emotion rather than reasoning.
- Do not make recurring characters into engagement mascots or attachment mechanisms.
- Do not assign stable moral/personality functions to visual types.
- Do not use panel density as decoration; each frame transition must have an information/pacing function.
- Do not assume findings from teacher education transfer directly to children.
- Do not assume findings from Japanese samples generalise unchanged to Italian pupils.

## Research expansion required

The next Japanese-language search should deepen four strands separately:

- **sequential visual grammar / panel composition and child comprehension**;
- **character design, identification and viewpoint-taking**;
- **educational manga and learning outcomes beyond interest**;
- **developmental/age differences and accessibility in visual narrative**.

For each strand, preserve source, population, method, outcome, limitation and explicit Atlas implication. Where evidence is aesthetic/theoretical rather than empirical, label it accordingly.

## Current decision impact

This research does **not** select a manga style for Atlas.

It supports continuing the G1 hypothesis that Atlas Percorsi may benefit from a **coherent continuous narrative world whose characters, frames and local metaphors each have explicit cognitive functions**.

The next design experiment should therefore test not “manga versus non-manga”, but whether **stable characters + selective frame changes + viewpoint dialogue** can reduce image replacement and improve causal/perspective understanding compared with the current full-illustration sequence.
