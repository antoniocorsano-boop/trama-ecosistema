# Atlas Percorsi — Narrative Presence and Character Function Screening v1

**Status:** RESEARCH_SCREENING / DESIGN_INPUT / NOT_RUNTIME_AUTHORIZATION  
**Target:** first-cycle learner-facing Percorsi  
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## Research question

When should characters, story and social/narrative presence be treated as part of the learning architecture rather than decoration?

## Screened evidence

### Pedagogical agents

Schroeder, Adesope & Gilbert (2013), *How Effective are Pedagogical Agents for Learning? A Meta-Analytic Review*.

- 43 studies / 3,088 participants;
- pedagogical agents showed a small but statistically detectable benefit for learning;
- effects varied by design/context;
- K-12 learners benefited more than post-secondary learners.

Design consequence:
- visible characters can support learning when they carry instructional/social function;
- this does **not** justify decorative mascots;
- character presence should reduce ambiguity, carry viewpoint/information, scaffold action or make consequences socially intelligible.

### Social cues in AI-powered pedagogical agents

Zhang et al. (2025), *Effects of social cues of artificial intelligence-powered pedagogical agents: A multilevel meta-analysis*, Educational Research Review 49, 100746.

- 33 experimental studies;
- positive effects were reported across cognitive, affective and behavioural learning outcomes;
- no statistically detectable increase in cognitive load was found overall;
- visual representation and anthropomorphic social cues were among the moderators.

Design consequence:
- social presence can be useful, especially when a visible actor has a clear functional role;
- Atlas should not infer that “more human-like” is always better;
- character/social cues must remain bounded by privacy, child safety and attention proportionality.

### Narrative learning

Gasser, Dammert & Murphy (2022), *How Do Children Socially Learn from Narrative Fiction?*, Educational Psychology Review.

- narrative learning can support expressive/lived-through and critical-analytic engagement;
- intentional stance design matters;
- narrative should shape interpretation and viewpoint, not simply decorate content.

Design consequence:
- a story should create a reason for the learner's action;
- characters may distribute information, perspective, uncertainty and consequence;
- narrative must not become a prose preface detached from the task.

## Synthesis

For first-cycle Percorsi, **narrative presence is required when the cognitive task depends on consequence, uncertainty, perspective, decision, social meaning or causality**.

A narrative-present pathway should provide:

1. **someone** — one or more actors with bounded roles;
2. **somewhere** — a visible world/location or situation;
3. **something wanted** — a goal meaningful inside that world;
4. **something changed** — an event/problem that creates the task;
5. **learner role** — why the learner is being asked to act;
6. **information carriers** — who/what provides partial evidence;
7. **consequence** — what becomes different after a learner action;
8. **continuity** — the same world/characters persist across scenes unless transfer deliberately changes context.

## Anti-pattern

A pathway has **thin narrative skin** when:
- a story is stated only in a paragraph;
- characters are absent or interchangeable;
- all meaningful action happens on generic cards/buttons;
- removing names/setting changes nothing about the task;
- the learner cannot explain why their action matters to anyone inside the experience.

Thin narrative skin fails narrative presence.

## Boundary

Characters are not required in every possible Percorso. They are required when they materially help carry the cognitive/narrative structure. Where omitted, the dossier must explain what other representation provides agency, continuity, viewpoint and consequence.
