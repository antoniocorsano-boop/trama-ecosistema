## Method warning

## C3 — Error and feedback

### Screened direction

Formative-feedback literature supports feedback that reduces the gap between current and intended performance when it provides information that can guide a next action. Feedback effects are heterogeneous: feedback can help, do little, or harm depending on content, timing, framing, task and learner conditions.

### Atlas consequence

An error must not trigger identity judgement. A useful response should preferentially expose one or more of:

- relevant evidence the learner overlooked;
- a consequence of the current choice;
- a constraint not yet satisfied;
- a comparison or alternative strategy;
- a recoverable next action.

Atlas should distinguish **informational feedback** from praise/blame and from reward animation. The pathway should allow revision where revision is pedagogically coherent.

### Claim boundary

The evidence does not justify a universal feedback formula, timing, number of attempts or amount of explanation for every age/task. Feedback design remains pathway- and developmental-band-specific.

## C11 — Relational/anthropomorphic agents

### Screened direction

Human–robot and conversational-agent research shows that people can respond socially to artificial agents; anthropomorphic, empathic and self-disclosing cues can alter trust, perceived social presence and willingness to disclose. The literature is heterogeneous and much is adult-focused. Child–robot work nevertheless makes the relational dimension relevant to minors.

### Precautionary interpretation

For Atlas, a character can be pedagogically useful without being represented as a confidant, therapist or reciprocal intimate relationship. Because relational cues can change trust/disclosure behaviour, the absence of definitive child-specific harm thresholds is **not** permission to maximise anthropomorphism or intimacy.

### Atlas consequence

Child-facing agents/characters must:

- remain clearly bounded educational/narrative entities;
- avoid claims of human-like inner experience or privileged psychological understanding of the learner;
- avoid reciprocal personal self-disclosure designed to elicit disclosure from the child;
- avoid requests for secrets, intimate biography, health, distress, family/relationship details or identity-sensitive information;
- avoid exclusivity language and dependency framing;
- avoid presenting themselves as therapists, counsellors or substitute attachment figures;
- avoid using empathy cues as a retention mechanism;
- keep task-relevant support separable from relational persuasion.

### Evidence status

The **privacy/no-disclosure boundary** is already strongly supported by child-rights and data-minimisation governance. The more specific relational-agent restrictions are currently **precautionary + evidence-informed**, pending stronger child-specific evidence on LLM/agent interaction.

## C16 — Emotion and social-emotional learning

### Screened direction

Research on emotion regulation and school-based social-emotional learning supports a relationship between emotional processes, learning and school functioning, and reports benefits from some structured programmes. Effects vary by programme, context, implementation, outcome and population.

### Atlas consequence

Atlas may use fictional scenarios to represent uncertainty, frustration, disagreement, competing goals, perspective-taking and recovery after mistakes. It should not infer a child's emotional state from task choices or claim that completing a pathway develops a broad social-emotional trait.

Emotional design should support the competence task by:

- providing psychological distance through fictional/hypothetical scenarios;
- allowing revision and recovery;
- avoiding humiliation and fear;
- keeping challenge bounded and intelligible;
- allowing exit from uncomfortable scenarios without punishment;
- separating emotional tone from diagnostic inference.

### Claim boundary

A pathway can provide an opportunity to practise a bounded social/emotional decision or regulation strategy. It cannot, from pathway completion alone, claim that the learner is empathic, resilient, emotionally regulated or socially competent.

## Cross-rule decision

C3, C11 and C16 should be interpreted together:

`error → informative consequence → safe opportunity to reconsider → task-bound reflection`

not:

`error → emotional pressure → agent intimacy/reassurance → personal disclosure → hidden trait inference`.

This distinction is constitutional for child-facing Percorsi.
