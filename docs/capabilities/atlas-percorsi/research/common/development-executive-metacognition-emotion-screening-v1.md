# Atlas Percorsi — Development, Executive Functions, Metacognition and Emotion Screening v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery / industrialisation  
**Status:** ACTIVE SCREENING  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED  
**Date:** 2026-09-25

## Research question

How should developmental evidence on executive functions, metacognition/self-regulated learning and emotion constrain Atlas Percorsi for primary and lower-secondary learners, without turning an educational tool into psychological profiling?

## Search-quality note

The first broad OpenAlex query for executive functions returned substantial semantic noise and unrelated highly cited records. It is retained as a methodological warning: citation rank and keyword presence are not sufficient evidence screening. Follow-up searches therefore used more specific educational/developmental terms and identified relevant literature on executive functions, cognitive flexibility, metacognition, self-regulation and emotion in school-age populations.

No search result becomes an Atlas rule merely by appearing in a result set.

## Core developmental position

Atlas Percorsi should treat transversal competences as **developing, context-sensitive performances that can be supported and practised**, not as fixed attributes of a child.

This has two consequences:

1. the system may design opportunities to practise planning, inhibition, working-memory support, flexible strategy use, monitoring, reflection and emotion-aware decision making;
2. it must not infer or store stable psychological/personality labels from a minor's responses.

## Executive functions — screened direction

### Construct boundary

Executive-function literature commonly distinguishes interrelated processes including inhibitory control, working memory and cognitive flexibility, with more complex planning/reasoning building on these capacities. Development continues through childhood and adolescence and interacts with context, knowledge and task demands.

**Permitted design inference:** Percorsi may reduce avoidable demands and create bounded opportunities to practise pausing, holding/revisiting relevant information, switching perspective or strategy, planning and checking.

**Not permitted:** diagnosing an executive-function weakness from pathway behaviour or treating one digital task as a valid general measure of executive functioning.

### Design consequences

- externalise information that need not be held in memory;
- make goals and constraints inspectable;
- allow revision before commitment where the competence permits it;
- use comparison and perspective change deliberately;
- distinguish productive challenge from interface-induced executive burden;
- provide scaffolds that can fade rather than permanent prompting;
- avoid speed as a default proxy for competence.

## Metacognition and self-regulation — screened direction

Metacognitive and self-regulated learning traditions support attention to planning, monitoring, strategy selection and evaluation. Evidence also indicates that these processes can be taught/supported, including in younger learners, but measurement is non-trivial and developmental appropriateness matters.

### Candidate Percorsi cycle

`goal/mission → choose or predict → act → inspect consequence/evidence → compare with intention → revise strategy → articulate what helped`

This is a candidate functional grammar, not a mandatory screen sequence.

### Reflection rule

Reflection should be **task-bound, concrete and age-appropriate**. Prefer prompts such as:

- What information helped you decide?
- What changed your mind?
- Which clue would you check first next time?
- Is there another strategy that could work here?

Avoid prompts that pressure the child to disclose intimate experiences, family situations, distress, identity or relationships.

The system should permit fictional/hypothetical reflection and, where pedagogically adequate, non-verbal or low-text responses.

## Emotion — screened direction

Emotion and emotion regulation are relevant to learning, attention, decision making and social interaction. School-based social-emotional programmes show potentially beneficial outcomes, but effects depend on programme, context, implementation and measurement. Atlas must therefore avoid turning broad SEL findings into claims about an individual child.

### Design consequences

Narrative can safely externalise emotional/social situations through fictional characters and scenarios. This can create distance between the learner and the problem while allowing perspective-taking and consequence reasoning.

A character may represent uncertainty, frustration, disagreement, competing goals or changing evidence when these states are necessary to the competence task. The character must not become a pseudo-therapist or solicit confidential disclosure.

### Emotional-safety boundary

Percorsi should:

- avoid shame, humiliation and fear as performance mechanisms;
- avoid loss-framed streaks and emotional pressure to continue;
- avoid moralising a single incorrect choice into a judgement of the learner;
- allow recovery and revision after error;
- frame errors as information for the task;
- provide exits from emotionally uncomfortable fictional situations;
- avoid simulating exclusive intimacy or dependency between child and character/system.

## From 'self' to competence without profiling

The project's idea of starting from the self can be retained if `self` means **agency and reflective awareness in the current task**, not collection of personal biography.

Safe interpretation:

`I notice → I choose → I see a consequence → I reconsider → I can explain/adapt my strategy`

Unsafe interpretation:

`the system learns who I am → classifies my personality/emotions → predicts my traits → stores a profile`

Atlas Percorsi adopts the first interpretation.

## Age and developmental differentiation

A single reflective/narrative grammar should not be assumed across the whole first cycle.

Candidate differentiation to validate:

### Earlier primary
- concrete situations and visible consequences;
- short decision chains;
- externalised memory supports;
- limited simultaneous alternatives;
- reflection through choice, ordering, matching, drawing/iconic representation or short language.

### Later primary
- explicit comparison of strategies;
- increasing perspective-taking;
- simple planning and revision;
- explanation of evidence and reasons;
- controlled contextual variation.

### Lower secondary
- more complex trade-offs and delayed consequences;
- competing evidence and viewpoints;
- stronger strategy justification;
- planning across multiple steps;
- metacognitive comparison across contexts.

These are **candidate developmental bands**, not fixed age norms or diagnostic expectations.

## Measurement boundary

For child-facing Percorsi, evidence should primarily describe **task performance**:

- strategy selected;
- evidence used;
- revision after new information;
- successful/unsuccessful adaptation to changed context;
- explanation produced;
- support required within the task.

Do not transform these into stable labels such as `self-regulated`, `impulsive`, `empathetic`, `resilient`, `mature`, `anxious`, `poor collaborator` or equivalent.

No hidden psychometric score should be created merely because interaction data make it technically possible.

## Privacy consequence

The strongest privacy architecture remains data minimisation by design:

- no student account required;
- no need for real name or personal biography;
- no behavioural profile for future targeting;
- no cross-pathway psychological dossier;
- downloadable/local artefacts may preserve learner work without requiring server-side identity;
- any future telemetry proposal requires separate governance, necessity proof and child-safety/privacy review.

## Connection to narrative and visual design

Developmental support strengthens the case for **functional recurring characters and stable visual anchors**, but not for a universal mascot. Recurrence is useful when it reduces re-orientation cost or makes a viewpoint/strategy recognisable.

Different pathways may therefore use different narrative traditions while preserving common safeguards. Character, manga/comic, animation, simulation or theatre devices must be justified by the competence function and developmental band.

## Claims explicitly not authorised

This screening does not authorise:

- personality assessment;
- emotional diagnosis;
- automated psychological inference;
- a universal SEL score;
- fixed developmental expectations for every child of a given age;
- claims that executive-function training automatically transfers broadly;
- claims that one narrative style is developmentally optimal;
- collecting personal disclosures to make reflection 'authentic';
- treating engagement or compliance as self-regulation.

## Evidence normalisation requirements

Future evidence records must distinguish:

- construct studied;
- learner age/developmental range;
- school/context;
- intervention versus correlational evidence;
- near versus broader transfer;
- measurement method;
- duration/follow-up;
- implementation conditions;
- limitations;
- permitted product inference.

Priority evidence classes:

1. executive-function development and school learning;
2. executive-function intervention transfer and limits;
3. metacognition/SRL in primary school;
4. metacognition measurement in children/adolescents;
5. collaborative/peer-supported metacognition;
6. emotion regulation and academic learning;
7. school-based SEL meta-analyses and implementation moderators;
8. developmental appropriateness of reflection prompts;
9. privacy/ethics of behavioural inference about minors.

## Current decision

Promote the following to **G1 design constraints**:

- developmental appropriateness;
- task-bound metacognitive reflection;
- external support for avoidable memory/executive demands;
- revision/recovery as part of learning;
- fictional/hypothetical emotional distance where useful;
- task-performance evidence rather than trait inference;
- strict separation between educational interaction and psychological profiling.

Do **not** promote a psychometric model, universal developmental scale or fixed pathway grammar.

## Next dependency

The next consolidation step should combine transfer, cognitive-load/multimedia and developmental findings into a single **Child-Safe Learning & Experience Constitution** for Atlas Percorsi. That constitution should define non-negotiable product constraints while leaving pathway-specific narrative grammars and visual identities free to vary within evidence-based boundaries.
