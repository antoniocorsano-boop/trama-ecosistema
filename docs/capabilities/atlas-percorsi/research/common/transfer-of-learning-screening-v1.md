# Atlas Percorsi — Transfer of Learning Screening v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery / industrialisation  
**Status:** ACTIVE SCREENING  
**Runtime:** NOT_AUTHORIZED  
**Date:** 2026-09-25

## Research question

What does mature research on transfer of learning justify for the design and validation of Atlas Percorsi, especially for transversal competences in primary and lower-secondary learners?

## Method note

Broad database queries produced substantial semantic noise because `transfer learning` is also a machine-learning term. This screening therefore treats generic search output only as discovery material and gives greater weight to transfer-specific educational literature and child/school-context studies. Search-result presence is not evidence adoption.

## Working construct

For Percorsi, transfer must not be treated as a binary label. The relevant question is whether a learner can recognise, select, adapt and use a strategy or competence when surface features, task demands, representation, social context or domain change.

The traditional near/far distinction remains useful descriptively, but distance must be operationalised for each pathway rather than assumed.

## Screened evidence directions

### 1. Transfer is not automatic — SUPPORTED direction

The literature consistently treats transfer as a central but difficult educational outcome. Performance in the learning context cannot by itself establish performance in a changed context.

**Design consequence:** every competence pathway that claims transfer must contain at least one changed-context performance opportunity and must define what changed.

### 2. Similarity matters, but surface similarity is insufficient — SUPPORTED/CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

Nearer tasks generally provide more retrieval cues and structural overlap. Farther transfer requires learners to recognise deeper relations despite contextual change.

**Design consequence:** Percorsi should vary contexts progressively rather than merely repeat the same interaction with new decoration.

### 3. Abstraction and explicit comparison are plausible transfer supports — SUPPORTED direction, implementation uncertain

Research traditions on transfer, analogical learning and instruction support making underlying structure noticeable, comparing cases and helping learners distinguish relevant from incidental features.

**Design consequence:** candidate pathway moves include comparing two situations, naming or representing a strategy, explaining why it applies, and then applying it in a differently presented situation. Exact sequencing remains a testable hypothesis.

### 4. Knowledge and domain structure remain important — SUPPORTED direction

General labels such as critical thinking or problem solving should not be treated as context-free powers. Transfer depends partly on what the learner knows and on recognising relevant structure in the target situation.

**Design consequence:** a transversal competence pathway must not become content-free puzzles. Scenarios need sufficient domain/context knowledge or embedded support to make reasoning possible.

### 5. Far-transfer claims require stronger evidence — SUPPORTED methodological rule

As source and target contexts diverge, alternative explanations increase and evidence becomes harder to interpret. A single successful scenario cannot justify a broad competence claim.

**Design consequence:** Atlas must use calibrated language. Evidence of success in one changed scenario may support a local performance statement, not a global statement about the child.

## Age/context boundary

The current search contains relevant work involving primary pupils and childhood critical-thinking development, but much transfer literature concerns adults, university students or workplace learning. Therefore:

- adult/university findings may inform hypotheses;
- they cannot silently become child-facing requirements;
- evidence packets must record age, educational setting, task, intervention duration and outcome type;
- child-specific evidence receives separate status in later synthesis.

## Consequences for transversal competence design

A candidate Percorsi progression is:

`encounter → supported practice → comparison/structure noticing → contextual variation → independent selection/adaptation → changed-context performance → non-sensitive reflection`

This is a **design hypothesis**, not a universal pedagogical law.

### Required validation dimensions

For each transfer task record:

1. **source context** — where the strategy was learned/practised;
2. **target context** — where it is tested;
3. **changed dimensions** — surface, representation, actors, domain, constraints, timing, collaboration, etc.;
4. **invariant structure** — what relation/strategy is intended to transfer;
5. **support level** — prompt, hint, scaffold or independent use;
6. **observable action** — what the learner actually does;
7. **claim ceiling** — strongest interpretation the evidence permits.

## Child-safe assessment boundary

Transfer evidence must concern the performed task, not inferred identity or personality. Percorsi must not generate statements such as `you are impulsive`, `you lack empathy`, `you are a poor collaborator`, or equivalent psychological/personality profiles.

No transfer task should require disclosure of private family circumstances, health, relationships, distress, identity, or other sensitive personal information. Reflection should permit fictional, hypothetical or task-bound responses.

## Visual/narrative consequence

Changing the artwork while preserving an identical task is not meaningful transfer. Conversely, changing every representational feature at once can introduce irrelevant difficulty.

Narrative and visual variation should therefore be controlled as an experimental/design variable:

- retain enough continuity for orientation;
- vary selected contextual features deliberately;
- preserve the deep relation being exercised;
- avoid decorative novelty that consumes attention without serving the competence.

This rule connects transfer research directly to later cognitive-load, attention and multimedia screening.

## Claims explicitly not authorised

Current evidence does **not** authorise:

- a universal number of contexts needed to prove transfer;
- a universal near/far threshold;
- certification of transversal competence from pathway completion;
- inference of stable personal traits from choices;
- the claim that gamification, comics, manga, animation or narrative intrinsically increase transfer;
- treating engagement, time-on-task, points or accuracy in repeated identical tasks as transfer.

## Evidence classes for next pass

The next evidence normalisation should create records for:

- foundational transfer frameworks;
- situated cognition/context dependence;
- analogical comparison and structural abstraction;
- critical-thinking transfer;
- primary/lower-secondary transfer studies;
- transfer measurement methodology;
- negative/null transfer findings.

Each record must capture population, context, task, outcome, distance/change definition, limitations and permitted design inference.

## Current decision

**Transfer is promoted from an implicit aspiration to an explicit design-and-validation dimension of Atlas Percorsi.**

What is promoted is the requirement to design and test for changed-context performance—not any specific instructional technique or claim that transfer has occurred.

## Next research dependency

Proceed with cognitive load, working memory, attention and multimedia learning, because contextual variation and visual/narrative richness can either support structural recognition or create extraneous processing. Those literatures must constrain the transfer design before narrative grammars are promoted.
