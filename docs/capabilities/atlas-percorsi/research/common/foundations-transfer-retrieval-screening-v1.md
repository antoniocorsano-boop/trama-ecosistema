## Research question

Which mature findings about retrieval, spacing, variation and transfer can legitimately inform a competence-practice architecture for Atlas Percorsi, especially for primary and lower-secondary learners?

## Why this precedes medium/style research

Percorsi must not equate attractive narrative, completion or engagement with competence. Before selecting manga, anime, simulation, inquiry or other experiential grammars, the common architecture needs evidence about how learning is retained, retrieved under changed conditions and transferred.

## Search quality finding

The first broad OpenAlex query (`learning transfer retrieval practice`) produced substantial semantic noise, including unrelated uses of “transfer learning”. This is itself a documented methodological result: broad keyword searches are not sufficient for this programme.

A subsequent targeted Scholar query around **test-enhanced learning / retrieval practice / education** produced a materially more relevant candidate set, including review-oriented and classroom-oriented work.

**Procedure consequence:** future evidence packets use concept-specific terms, known theory labels, backward/forward citation checks and explicit screening. Search ranking/citation count alone is never an inclusion criterion.

A newly surfaced systematic review candidate.

**Screening need:** because it is recent, inspect protocol, included educational levels, outcome definitions and whether testing frequency is separable from feedback/instructional changes.

## Provisional design implications

These are **UNCERTAIN pending screening**, not requirements:

1. Percorsi should likely create opportunities to retrieve/use a strategy after initial recognition rather than merely repeat exposition.
2. Changed contexts may be necessary to test transfer rather than visual memory for the original scene.
3. Repeated practice should vary surface features while preserving the target cognitive relation.
4. Feedback/consequence should support learning without turning every retrieval into scoring or reward.
5. Delayed or later retrieval may matter; immediate success alone should not be interpreted as durable competence.

## Claims explicitly NOT authorised by this screening

- retrieval practice automatically creates transversal competence;
- quizzes are the preferred Percorsi interaction;
- more frequent testing is always better;
- engagement predicts transfer;
- a strategy recognised in one narrative transfers to another domain;
- game completion is evidence of competence.

## Required next screening

1. Locate mature systematic reviews/meta-analyses specifically on retrieval practice in educational settings.
2. Separate retention from near transfer and far transfer outcomes.
3. Extract child/adolescent evidence separately from university/adult evidence.
4. Screen spacing/interleaving/variation literature as related but distinct mechanisms.
5. Screen transfer-of-learning reviews directly, not only retrieval literature.
6. Connect only screened implications to the competence-progression model.
