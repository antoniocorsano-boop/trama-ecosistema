# CAP-ATLAS-PERCORSI — Evidence-First Research Program v1

**Gate:** G1 — Discovery
**Status:** GOVERNED_RESEARCH_PROGRAM / OPEN
**Purpose:** ensure that Atlas Percorsi narrative/experiential design is derived from mature evidence and documented theory rather than invented from interface ideas.

## Core rule

Atlas Percorsi does not invent a narrative, metaphor, character system or interaction grammar first and search for justification later.

The governed order is:

`COMPETENCE QUESTION → MATURE EVIDENCE → THEORY/MECHANISM → AGE/CONTEXT LIMITS → DESIGN HYPOTHESES → CONTROLLED PROTOTYPE → VALIDATION`

Design may generate hypotheses, but it cannot convert plausibility into evidence.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, where available:

1. systematic reviews / meta-analyses / evidence syntheses;
2. mature theoretical frameworks with substantial empirical testing;
3. replicated or convergent empirical studies;
4. high-quality age/context-specific studies;
5. design-based research and evaluated educational interventions;
6. exploratory studies and case studies;
7. professional/design precedents as inspiration only.

Popularity, commercial success, aesthetic quality and engagement are not substitutes for learning evidence.

## Cross-cultural evidence rule

Japanese research on manga/anime is a valuable strand, but not a privileged default. Separate:

- research conducted with Japanese populations;
- research about Japanese manga/anime as media;
- general comics/animation research;
- cross-cultural comparisons.

Never assume direct transfer to Italian primary/lower-secondary learners without documenting population, context, cultural literacy and limitations.

## Evidence record minimum

Every material source must record:

- bibliographic identifier;
- study/review type;
- population and age;
- country/cultural context;
- sample/setting where available;
- intervention/material;
- comparison/baseline where relevant;
- outcomes actually measured;
- supported claim;
- claims NOT supported;
- limitations;
- applicability to Atlas;
- applicable grammar IDs;
- applicable competence territories;
- confidence/uncertainty;
- design consequence;
- date reviewed.

## Research-to-design rule

For each design decision preserve:

`EVIDENCE REF(S) → MECHANISM/INTERPRETATION → UNCERTAINTY → DESIGN CONSEQUENCE → TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS`

Example:

- evidence may support learner-controlled segmentation in multimedia learning;
- interpretation: externally imposed rapid transitions can burden processing;
- uncertainty: effect size/context may differ by task and age;
- consequence: Atlas should default to learner-controlled scene progression;
- hypothesis: controlled progression preserves comprehension better than automatic scene turnover in this pathway.

This chain prevents citation laundering: a source cannot be cited for a design claim it did not test.

## Negative evidence and null results

Record evidence that challenges a preferred design, including null effects, age limitations and contexts where an approach performs poorly. A grammar registry entry must contain risks and contraindications, not only benefits.

## Search protocol

For a new research question:

1. formulate the pedagogical/cognitive question before keywords;
2. search reviews/meta-analyses first;
3. identify canonical theories/frameworks and their critiques;
4. search age-specific evidence;
5. search context/culture-specific evidence;
6. search recent updates/replications;
7. extract limitations and contradictory findings;
8. map only supported implications to Atlas;
9. mark unresolved questions;
10. avoid further searching once evidence saturation is sufficient for the decision, unless risk level requires more.

## Governance consequence

The experiential-grammar registry cannot promote a grammar from `research` to a stronger status merely because a prototype is attractive or coherent. Promotion requires an evidence packet plus pathway-specific validation appropriate to the claim.

Likewise, an AI-generated narrative cannot become canonical content without evidence linkage, safeguard review and human editorial/pedagogical review.

## Repository consequence

The repository—not chat history—must preserve:

- search questions and dates;
- screened evidence records;
- evidence syntheses;
- rejected/contradictory evidence;
- research reasoning;
- grammar implications;
- pathway-specific evidence links;
- decision records.

This makes the research process reproducible and allows future pathway authoring to reuse mature knowledge instead of repeatedly asking an AI to reconstruct it.
